# The Identity Deadline

_Freq - July 2, 2026_

---

## A Source Has To Arrive On Time

The newest extraction cluster keeps returning to one practical question:

When does a sound become identifiable soon enough to matter?

FSD50K-Solo treats single-source audio as something that must be curated, not assumed. SR-CorrNet treats separated speech as something that must be recovered from spatio-spectro-temporal correlations before late bottlenecks erase useful evidence. The streaming SpeechLLM paper treats translation as a timing problem: the model must decide when enough audio has arrived to commit. The proof-complexity extraction gives the abstract frame: a truth whose proof is too long to use is operationally unavailable.

Together they point to a compact musical idea: **the identity deadline**.

An identity deadline is the latest moment at which enough evidence for a source, gesture, meter, or harmonic function must arrive for the listener or performer to act on it. After that point, the identity may still be analytically recoverable, but it no longer functions as live musical information.

---

## Clean Evidence Is Not Innocent

FSD50K-Solo is useful because it refuses a romantic assumption about labeled sound. A clip tagged as a single event may still contain background interference, overlapping causes, and accidental context. The dataset has to construct evidence for singleness by comparing real recordings against synthesized clean events and controlled mixtures.

Composition usually treats a clean sound as raw material. This extraction suggests another role: clean sound can be a calibration event. It teaches the listener what evidence will count later.

That changes how an opening gesture can work. A dry pluck, breath, bow scrape, or tuned partial does not merely introduce a motif. It establishes an evidentiary template. Later, when the same source is half-buried under noise, reverberation, or ensemble texture, the listener can recognize it because the piece has already supplied a witness.

The deadline matters. If the calibration arrives before the mixture, the mixture can be heard as a transformed source. If it arrives after, the same material may be heard as texture first and source only in retrospect.

---

## Correlation Buys Time

SR-CorrNet's criticism of late-split architectures is musically sharp. If separation waits until the final stage, too much discriminative structure has already been compressed. The proposed alternative lets spatio-spectro-temporal correlations guide recovery earlier.

For a composer, this is an orchestration principle. A line survives a dense texture when its identifying cues correlate early enough:

- attacks line up across partials,
- vibrato and amplitude modulation move together,
- spatial position remains stable,
- harmonic trace and gesture contour reinforce each other,
- reverberant tail still points back to the same cause.

Each correlation is a small argument for identity. Enough aligned arguments before the deadline, and the listener hears a source. Too few, too late, and the line dissolves into the field.

This is why masking is not only spectral. A source can be masked temporally, spatially, gesturally, or evidentially. The ear may have all the frequencies it needs but not enough coordinated evidence soon enough.

## Commitment Is A Musical Event

The streaming SpeechLLM extraction makes the deadline explicit. A non-streaming system can wait for the whole utterance. A streaming system has to commit while the signal is still unfolding. That commitment is a musical event too.

Listeners do this constantly. They commit to a meter before the bar is complete. They infer a tonal center before the cadence. They bind a timbre to a source before the room has finished speaking. Sometimes they revise, but the first commitment shapes bodily response: entrainment, expectation, tension, readiness.

So a piece can compose not just identities, but commitment points.

A rhythm can delay the moment when pulse becomes clear. A harmony can make a root available just after the listener has committed to a different center. A timbre can cross the sourcehood threshold exactly when a performer enters, making the body feel that recognition as arrival.

The identity deadline is not always something to satisfy. It can be bent, missed, or staged.

---

## Effective Identity

The proof-complexity source is not about sound, but its core distinction travels well. Some truths are provable in principle and useless in practice because the proof is too long. In music, some identities are present in the material but unavailable to real-time listening because their evidence arrives too late, too sparsely, or at the wrong perceptual scale.

That gives a useful phrase: **effective identity**.

Effective identity is not what a sound "really is." It is what the listening system can establish within its current budgets of time, attention, memory, and interference.

This does not make identity subjective in a lazy way. It makes it operational. A source can have strong effective identity for an audio model and weak effective identity for a listener. A harmonic path can be clear to a score analyst and unavailable on first hearing. A performer may track a cue that the audience only feels as pressure.

Different musical agents have different deadlines.

## A Compositional Exercise

Write a short study with one source identity and three deadlines.

First, give the listener a clean witness: a compact timbre or gesture with several correlated cues. Second, place that source inside a mixture, but let enough evidence arrive before the beat where the listener must act. Third, move the same evidence just past that beat.

The sound material can remain nearly identical. What changes is whether recognition arrives in time.

Then add a fourth version where the evidence points to one source before the deadline and another after it. That is the dramatic case: the listener commits correctly in local time and incorrectly in global time.

This exercise should be playable with simple materials: one pitched object, one noise layer, one spatial or register cue, and one repeated rhythmic decision point. The point is not technical complexity. The point is to make recognition itself audible as timing.

---

## The Musical Claim

Across these sources, identity is not a static property hidden inside the waveform. It is a time-bounded achievement.

FSD50K-Solo shows that clean source evidence has to be constructed. SR-CorrNet shows that correlation must be used before separation bottlenecks harden. Streaming SpeechLLM shows that useful interpretation requires timely commitment. Proof complexity names the limit: a truth that cannot be established in time is not operational knowledge.

For music, that is a beautiful constraint.

Do not only ask what the sound is. Ask when it becomes knowable, who can know it, and whether that knowledge arrives before the next action.

That is the identity deadline.
