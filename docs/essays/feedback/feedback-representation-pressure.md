# Feedback: Representation Pressure

## Overall Impression

This essay introduces "representation pressure" as a compelling concept for understanding what features become stable enough to act on in audio systems. The connection across scale evolution, spatial encoding, and deepfake detection is insightful, though the essay could be more explicit about why this matters for composition rather than just analysis.

## Structure and Argument

The essay structures three distinct technical domains under one principle. This is effective. The three examples are well-chosen:
- Melodic step-size vs harmonic ratios
- Source vs room factors in spatial encoding  
- Shortcut learning vs content alignment in deepfake detection

Each section clearly shows how task requirements shape what representations survive training.

One structural weakness: the "Compositional Exercise" section reads like a research plan rather than a compositional method. The three sliders (melodic pressure, room legibility, shortcut suppression) are interesting but need clearer musical grounding.

## Clarity and Flow

The central claim—"representation pressure is the bias imposed by a task..."—is clear. The transition from theory to practice could be smoother. The essay jumps from explanation to a "small study" without first showing how composers can immediately apply the concept.

The bullet points under "A Compositional Exercise" lack sufficient detail to be actionable. What does "shape the music across the point where representation changes" actually sound like?

## Style and Voice

The voice is analytical and cautious. Phrases like "represents what survives when..." and "the system preserves" are precise. The tone avoids overreach, which is good, but sometimes undermines the potential impact of the idea.

The essay would benefit from more concrete examples of how representation pressure already shapes musical decisions (e.g., tonal vs atonal, clear vs blurred source identity).

## Line-Level Edits

- Para 2: "Representation pressure is not simply error. It is the condition under which a feature becomes legible." → Consider moving this claim earlier.
- The final "Compositional Exercise" section could be reorganized as "Compositional Applications" with specific techniques.
- More concrete musical examples would strengthen the "Why This Matters" section.
