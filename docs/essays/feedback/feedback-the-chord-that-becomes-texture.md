# Feedback: The Chord That Becomes Texture
## Overall Impression

The core phrase—“pitch can be present without being sovereign”—gives the essay a strong perceptual thesis. It captures how dense spectra can retain pitch information while shifting attention toward mass, width, beating, and impact. The draft should resist treating that perceptual shift as a single threshold or as a direct consequence of voice count. Detuning, register, voicing, spectral envelope, phase, modulation, playback level, and listening task all influence whether an aggregate reads as chord or texture. “Threshold” is rhetorically effective but needs either experimental evidence or consistent qualification as a compositional metaphor.

The source base needs conventional citations and closer methodological description. A producer’s account, a pitch-strength study, a cross-cultural scale analysis, and an AI-generated bass anecdote do not have equal evidentiary weight. Separate reported measurement from interpretive extrapolation.

## Structure and Argument

The essay has a satisfying mirror structure: many oscillators fuse into one perceptual block, while one harmonic complex may yield multiple melodic implications. That is the strongest argumentative spine and should appear earlier. The scale-evolution section interrupts it because scale formation across cultures operates at a different explanatory level. Its claim about melodic constraints may be relevant to “pitch sovereignty,” but the connection is asserted rather than demonstrated. Either remove that section or explicitly explain how local interval accessibility changes the salience of harmony within a texture.

The passage from “pitch strength” to distributed “authority” needs care. Detuned stacking may weaken a single F0 percept, but it may also strengthen a chord’s pitch-class impression or create multiple strong pitch percepts. “Authority is distributed across…spectral width” mixes pitch evidence with non-pitch attributes. Define whether pitch strength is measured per tone, per partial, per frame, or perceptually rated.

The studio exercise is promising but does not isolate density: sparse lead, moderate unison, and dense chord stack may differ in pitch content, oscillator count, spectrum, loudness, and transient shape. Use the same chord and voicing throughout; vary detuning or number of layers one factor at a time; loudness-match renders; randomize presentation; and ask listeners separately about chord identification, perceived width, and textural mass. The harmonic-complex experiment likewise needs a precise method for moving upper partials without inadvertently creating actual additional fundamentals.

## Clarity and Flow

“Two eight-voice sawtooth layers” and the attribution to A. G. Cook need a direct, checkable source. If this is informal production commentary, identify its context and avoid making it stand as psychoacoustic evidence. The global scale claim should name the study’s dataset and clarify “performance-measured scales,” “harmonic-ratio ideals,” and whether one-to-three-semitone steps were observed or model-predicted.

The phrase “source count, pitch count, and perceived musical line count do not have to match” is clear and useful. Build definitions around it. In particular, distinguish physical source, synthesis oscillator, fundamental-frequency candidate, pitch class, and stream/voice; the current draft uses these categories interchangeably.

## Style and Voice

The voice balances technical and musical language well, and the recurring sovereignty metaphor provides cohesion. Use it sparingly enough that it remains explanatory rather than decorative. “Melody wins locally” and “Harmony…gets to claim it” personify explanatory factors in a way that oversimplifies a multi-causal study. More measured phrasing would make the central metaphor feel earned.

The final sentence is elegant, but “the texture temporarily permits us to name” implies that chord identity is arbitrary. A more defensible ending would emphasize a shift in perceptual priority rather than the disappearance of stable harmonic information.

## Line-Level Edits

- “technically casual but perceptually exact” overclaims exactness. Try: “technically informal but perceptually suggestive.”
- “push enough detuned sawtooths together and the chord stops presenting itself as a chord” should become: “dense detuned stacking can make textural attributes dominate chord identity.”
- “authority is distributed across detuned partials” is technically imprecise because detuned oscillators generate overlapping harmonic series. Consider: “periodicity cues compete with beating and spectral spread.”
- “Melody Wins Locally” could become “Local Melodic Constraints,” which avoids announcing a winner the cited evidence may not establish.
- “The thing we call ‘harmony’ may be a late-stage summary” is too broad. Try: “In this account, perceived harmonic organization may emerge from several lower-level constraints.”
- “a monophonic AI-generated bass-like sound reportedly supported two or more perceived melodic lines” needs the listening protocol, participant count, and definition of “supported”; otherwise label it an observation, not a result.
- “When should detuning turn harmony into percussion?” jumps to a new perceptual category. Replace “percussion” with “attack or impact” unless rhythmic/percussive perception was measured.
- “one oscillator” does not guarantee one harmonic source if synthesis or processing adds components. Specify the synthesis architecture.
