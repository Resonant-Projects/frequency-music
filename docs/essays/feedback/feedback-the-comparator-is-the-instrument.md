# Feedback: The Comparator Is the Instrument

**Overall Impression**  
This is a sharp, theoretically coherent essay that synthesizes technical papers into a compelling aesthetic principle. It effectively bridges signal processing and musical composition. However, the connection between the technical specifics and the musical applications could be tightened. The essay occasionally drifts into abstraction where a concrete example would anchor the argument more securely.

**Structure and Argument**  
The progression from specific papers (Prosodic ABX, Inter-Speaker Relative Cues, RIFT) to a general theory of perception ("perceptual structure is usually discovered through contrast") and finally to musical consequences is logical and effective. The argument builds well. 

The transition into "The Musical Consequence" is strong, but the examples provided ("pairing a stable version of a motif with a destabilized version," etc.) feel a bit generic. You've just explained complex signal processing concepts; grounding the musical application in a more specific, visceral example (e.g., how a specific rhythmic displacement functions, or a specific orchestrational contrast) would make the payoff hit harder.

**Clarity and Flow**  
The writing is generally clear and authoritative. You have a good instinct for punchy, declarative sentences ("That sounds modest. It is not.", "The comparator is doing the musical labor.").

However, the section on RIFT is slightly dense. While "fractional wavelet transforms" and "neighborhood of curvature" sound impressive, a brief, intuitive explanation of *why* multiple local frames reveal the trajectory better than a single frame would help readers without a DSP background.

**Style and Voice**  
The tone is appropriately analytical and confident. It reads like a thoughtful synthesis of research. You occasionally rely on rhetorical questions or slightly grand statements ("It changes the ontology of the task"), which work well in moderation but should be used sparingly to maintain their impact.
