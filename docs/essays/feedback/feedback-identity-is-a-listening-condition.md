# Feedback — "Identity Is A Listening Condition"

## Overall Impression
The essay's central reframe — identity as a permission granted by the listening apparatus, not a property of the signal — is genuinely productive, and the "Metadata Ear" section walks it through cleanly. But this is now the fourth or fifth piece in the series to lean on the same source cluster (SR-CorrNet, FSD50K-Solo, streaming SpeechLLM, anomalous sound detection). The reader who is following the series will feel diminishing returns; the essay does not acknowledge or navigate that overlap.

## Structure / Argument
- The opening question ("What does a listening system know before it listens?") is well-shaped, and the pivot to metadata is quick and effective.
- The "Metadata Ear" if/then paragraphs read cleanly as a logical progression but start to feel mechanical by the sixth if/then. Consider prose over the list form.
- "The Piece As Evaluation Protocol" is the most original section. The move from ML evaluation setup to compositional form is precise and useful. Extend this — it's where the essay earns its distinctiveness.
- The final "compositional handle" bulleted list is too long and reads as a spec for a tool rather than a claim about music. If the tool matters, describe it in prose; if it doesn't, cut.

## Clarity / Flow
- The essay is readable and well-signposted.
- "The ear is not just hearing the signal. It is hearing under conditions." — this is your thesis; it appears in the middle. Move it up.
- The section on "different identity supports disagree" is the most compositionally interesting moment and deserves its own section, not a five-bullet aside.

## Style / Voice
- Consistent with the series.
- Watch the reflex of ending sections with a single-sentence pronouncement — it's a mannerism now.
- "wonderfully concrete" is one of the few adjectives in this cluster that leans sentimental; earn it or trim.

## Line-Level Notes
- "Machine ID at test time. Single-source curation. Early separation. F0 contours under domain shift. Streaming commitment latency." — good rhythm, sharp enumeration.
- "The old benchmark condition says: evaluate each machine separately. The new condition says: merge them and remove test-time identity. Same recordings, different listening world." — genuinely useful; this is the essay's contribution.
- "Identity is one of the permissions." — the closer works, but the metaphor of permission has not been used consistently; you slip between "condition," "support," "handle," and "permission." Pick one and hold it.
