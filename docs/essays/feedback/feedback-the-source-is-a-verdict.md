# Feedback: The Source Is a Verdict

## Overall Impression

The essay is concise and has a compelling governing question: “who gets to decide when a sound has become one thing?” “Verdict,” however, implies a discrete, authoritative endpoint, while much source formation is probabilistic, hierarchical, and continuously revisable. The paper summaries do not establish a single adjudicating mechanism, and treating a room as a system that issues a verdict is a category error. Focus the essay on competing attributions by classifiers, separators, and listeners; use the room as evidence that complicates those attributions, not as a judge.

## Structure and Argument

FSD50K-Solo and SR-CorrNet give the essay a coherent first movement: an external curator decides which recordings count as class-pure, while a separator assigns mixture components to streams. These are genuinely different verdicts and could be contrasted more explicitly. What label or posterior does each output, at what threshold, and what can make it wrong?

The RIR section weakens the logic. A room physically transforms propagation; it does not “say” what a source is or treat evidence as one cause. Its reflections may encourage or disrupt perceptual grouping depending on delay, direction, and reverberation, but the essay needs auditory evidence for that connection. Also, text-conditioned RIR generation creates plausible transfer functions; it does not by itself demonstrate anything about source individuation.

The unconscious-perception source is potentially relevant but too vaguely reported. “If auditory processing can persist below conscious awareness” is a conditional premise, followed by “may keep sorting, adapting, or learning,” a list of different processes. State the paradigm, neural or behavioral measure, and exactly what persisted without awareness. Evidence for auditory processing is not automatically evidence for source formation. Avoid turning “the verdict can begin before it becomes conscious” into a conclusion unless source grouping was tested.

The definition paragraph overpopulates the category of decision-maker: a dataset curator, neural separator, performer, and listener can adopt classifications; a room cannot. Nor is “musically useful despite being wrong” explained—wrong relative to physical causation, annotation, or another listener? Define the reference standard.

The practical paragraph proposes machine/human disagreement, active reverberation, classifier failure, and bodily response. Choose one scenario and develop it into a testable compositional study. The current rapid list is imaginative but leaves “payoff” at the level of prompts. The final question is an effective ending; the sentence before it should acknowledge that there may be no final verdict, only task-relative assignments.

## Clarity and Flow

Define “clean source,” “source verdict,” and “one thing.” The essay alternates between a recording containing one labeled event, one physical excitation distributed through a room, and a perceptual object. These are not synonyms. A simple distinction among physical cause, signal component, and perceptual grouping would prevent the core metaphor from swallowing the analysis.

The flow is brisk, perhaps too brisk for four disciplines. Add factual detail to two primary cases and reduce the others to qualified complications. That would strengthen rather than slow the narrative.

## Style and Voice

The interrogative, composer-facing voice is strong, and “the clean source is useful, but it is not innocent” neatly exposes the politics of thresholds. Yet courtroom language encourages binary claims. Consider using “provisional verdict” consistently, or explain that a verdict can be revised as new acoustic evidence arrives.

Anthropomorphism is pervasive: “the room says,” “the listener’s body decides,” “the system treats.” It can be effective in an essay, but literal and metaphorical agents need clearer separation.

## Line-Level Edits

- “the clean source” as a “practical fiction” → “an operational ideal”; “fiction” risks implying that no recordings are genuinely dominated by one source.
- “A sound becomes usable when a classifier can say…” → “For this curation task, a recording becomes usable when its classifier score passes the chosen single-source threshold.”
- “which pieces of energy behave as if they belong together” → “which time-frequency features exhibit correlations the model uses for speaker-specific filter estimation.”
- “The room says…” → “Room propagation complicates the unit: one excitation reaches the listener through direct and reflected paths.”
- “Some layers of the nervous system may keep sorting, adapting, or learning” should name the demonstrated process and omit the others.
- “The system might be…a room” → remove “a room”; replace it with “an evaluation protocol” if needed.
- “The verdict can be…correct” requires a declared ground truth: physical emitter, dataset label, or perceptual consensus.
- “let the body follow a source contour” should specify the measurable bodily response or be framed explicitly as a compositional speculation.
