# Feedback: The Pitch Field
## Overall Impression

The essay offers an appealing alternative to pitch-as-coordinate, and its four regimes give readers a usable map. The problem is that “field” currently functions as a broad metaphor for four different things: transition probabilities in scales, phonological tone rules, oscillator-density effects, and spectral pitch multiplicity. The sources show that pitch is relational in several domains, but they do not yet establish one common field with comparable dimensions. The essay should either define the field abstractly—as a set of context-dependent constraints on pitch interpretation and motion—or present the four regimes as analogies whose mechanisms remain distinct.

## Structure and Argument

The scale discussion provides the most defensible foundation because it links an empirical distribution of step sizes to local melodic movement. Even there, the essay overreaches from prevalence to cognitive cost: a cross-cultural preference for one-to-three-semitone steps does not alone demonstrate that larger leaps are “expensive,” nor does it isolate singability, memory, and perception from instrument constraints, transcription conventions, corpus selection, or cultural transmission. Cite the source directly and separate the authors’ interpretation from yours.

The Yoruba section is valuable but needs more care. Lexical tone is not simply a musical example of contextual pitch. It is a linguistic contrast operating through relative fundamental-frequency contours, and the specific synthesizer’s diphone inventory is an engineering design rather than evidence for a general ontology of pitch. Explain what is source-specific and avoid making Yoruba a conceptual bridge to Western scale theory without qualification.

The supersaw and harmonic-complex sections then shift from horizontal motion to fusion and vertical spectrum. That shift can work if the essay explicitly says the field includes both temporal transition constraints and simultaneous grouping forces. Otherwise the four-regime list reads as taxonomy after the fact.

The proposed exercise promises “the same notes,” but converting a phrase into lexical contours changes the semantic and phonological frame, while emphasizing partials can alter perceived pitch. Reframe it as a comparison of a held symbolic scaffold under different relational constraints, not a controlled transformation that leaves pitch unchanged.

## Clarity and Flow

Define “pitch field” in operational terms near the beginning. “What neighboring motions, fusions, spellings, and partials are made available” introduces “spellings,” but notation never returns; remove it or develop it. “Local cost of motion” also needs a metric: statistical rarity, vocal effort, perceptual discriminability, or learned stylistic expectation?

The pitch-field controls are not parallel. “Unison density” is an audio parameter; “local motion cost” and “contour rule strength” are model-level abstractions; “partial audibility” is a perceptual outcome. Label them as candidate controls at different system layers rather than implying one unified interface.

## Style and Voice

The essay’s best lines—“A scale is…a contour machine” and “Pitch has not disappeared. It has become texture.”—are vivid because they condense an already intelligible mechanism. Elsewhere, beauty language substitutes for analysis (“beautiful inversion,” “principle rhymes”). Keep the lyricism but place it after the differences are made explicit.

The absence of date and section headings, unlike nearby essays, may be intentional, but the long uninterrupted sequence would benefit from two or three structural breaks. Those should organize the argument, not add more conceptual labels.

## Line-Level Edits

- “Across 1,314 scales from 96 countries” needs a conventional citation and a note on how scales and countries were sampled; country count is not equivalent to cultural representativeness.
- “near-universal preference” is likely too strong. Consider “a strong cross-corpus tendency in the authors’ sample.”
- “small motions are cheap” should become “small intervals are more frequent in the dataset”; the cost interpretation requires separate support.
- “TTSYoruba…uses a recorded inventory of 651 diphone units” should clarify whether 651 counts base units or tone-conditioned recordings and cite the system paper.
- “five tonal variants for consonant-vowel combinations” is difficult to reconcile with a three-level-tone system without explanation. Name the five categories.
- “A tone is a permitted trajectory” risks confusing a lexical tone category with its contextual phonetic realization. Try: “A lexical tone constrains a range of context-sensitive F0 realizations.”
- “until chord identity can blur” needs either listening evidence or attribution to the extraction; detuning does not inevitably erase chord identity.
- “single residue pitch” is undefined. If you mean missing-fundamental perception, say so.
- “the acoustic world that proves it” introduces proof language not developed here. Replace with “the acoustic context through which it is heard.”
- “speech, cloud, and polyphony” is rhetorically neat but technically mismatched. Specify “speech-like contouring, fused detuned texture, and partial-led multipitch implication.”
