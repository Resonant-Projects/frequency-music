# Feedback: The Carrier Decides: Composing What the Medium Lets Through
## Overall Impression

The essay has a useful compositional intuition—transport and transformation conditions can become musical material—but “carrier” expands until it covers nearly every mediating factor: room acoustics, transformers, codecs, time windows, prosody, pressure, and even a thermodynamic path. Once the term includes physical medium, signal processor, metadata, segmentation policy, and environmental response, the central claim becomes difficult to falsify. The draft should distinguish at least three things: transmission channels, transformations, and contextual constraints. Its strongest, defensible claim is not that “the carrier decides,” but that a representation or transmission path selectively preserves and alters features. That formulation retains the musical force without implying agency or a single mechanism.

Factual precision needs substantial work. The summaries cite source labels but give readers no authors, venues, links, dates, or methodological limits. Claims about ice phases, speech annotation, UAF latency and information loss, and infrasound all need source-specific wording rather than being recruited immediately into the metaphor.

## Structure and Argument

The progression from scientific examples to a studio hypothesis is clear, but the evidence section behaves as an anthology rather than an argument. The ice example establishes path dependence, not a “carrier.” Balalaika concerns auxiliary linguistic conditioning, which is closer to side information than transport. A 600 ms processing chunk is a temporal receptive field or interface constraint. A transformer is genuinely in the signal path. LoRa is a communications carrier. These examples could support a taxonomy, but they do not presently establish one shared causal principle.

Reorganize “Carriers With Bias” around explicit categories and state what each comparison can and cannot show. The paragraph conceding that the systems are not equivalent arrives too late; it should frame the examples before the analogies accumulate. The DAW test is a welcome attempt at falsifiability, yet its three versions change multiple variables simultaneously—saturation, low-frequency content, segmentation, filtering, and bandwidth—so it cannot isolate “carrier” as the cause. A stronger experiment would hold source, level, duration, and spectral balance as constant as practicable, manipulate one mediation variable at a time, and predefine listener questions.

The ending returns effectively to the essay’s governing image, but “when a carrier lets it pass through” overstates mediation as permission. Conclude with selective preservation: composition includes choosing what a path reveals, masks, or transforms.

## Clarity and Flow

Define “content” and “carrier” before relying on them. In audio engineering, content cannot always be cleanly separated from medium: transformer saturation adds harmonics, a room convolves a signal, and prosody is part of speech rather than a vessel around words. The phrase “route by which energy is carried” also does not fit token annotations or model architecture.

Several transitions use rhetorical equivalence where a precise bridge is needed: “That is already a compositional idea,” “UAF reaches a related conclusion,” and “Even the Focusrite ISA note belongs here.” Replace these with the exact shared relation—path dependence, conditioning, bandwidth limitation, or nonlinear coloration. The repetition of “The same…” in “Carrier Counterpoint” has rhythmic energy but produces a list of unlike manipulations. Trim it to two examples and explain the perceptual variable each tests.

## Style and Voice

The voice is confident, compact, and musically literate. Preserve the short declarative turns, especially “pitch,” “intelligibility,” and “pressure” as separate compositional concerns. However, the aphoristic style sometimes converts hypotheses into laws. “The carrier decides,” “meaning leaks away,” and “That makes the carrier audible as form” should be marked as proposals rather than conclusions.

Avoid importing scientific authority merely to intensify a studio observation. The infrasound paragraph is admirably cautious, but “sub-audible pressure layer that only modulates the room and body” is still physiologically loaded and potentially unsafe. Specify frequency and playback constraints, cite evidence, and avoid encouraging infrasonic reproduction without a safety basis.

## Line-Level Edits

- “Under Ostwald's step rule, it moves toward the nearest accessible phase” is too categorical and “nearest” is undefined. Consider: “Ostwald’s step rule describes a tendency for transformations to pass through metastable states before reaching a more stable phase; the cited experiment should be summarized with its actual pressure path and limits.”
- “The prosodic carrier changes how the signal can be reconstructed” misclassifies annotations. Try: “Prosodic and phonemic annotations provide conditioning information that can improve reconstruction.”
- “meaning leaks away between stages” is vivid but unsupported. Replace with: “cascaded stages can propagate errors and discard information relevant to later tasks.”
- “The same bass tone heard as audible pitch is different from a sub-audible pressure layer” compares different signals, not carriers. Try: “Moving energy below the pitch-salient range changes the perceptual role of the layer.”
- “one loudness target” is insufficient experimental control because saturation and filtering change perceived loudness. Specify loudness matching after processing and note that spectral matching may be necessary.
- “above the sub-audible danger zone” is undefined and misleading. Use a stated audible frequency range and safe monitoring level, or remove the sub-bass condition.
- “It becomes real when a carrier lets it pass through” could become: “It becomes audible in the particular form a signal path can preserve.”
