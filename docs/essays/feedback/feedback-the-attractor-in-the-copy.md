# Feedback: The Attractor in the Copy: What Iterative Degradation Reveals About Sound

## Overall Impression

This essay explores how iterative processing (like the I2D framework for TTS evaluation) reveals the intrinsic structure ("attractor") of an audio channel. The connection between modern AI speech evaluation and Alvin Lucier's _I Am Sitting in a Room_ is a brilliant piece of historical and technical synthesis.

## Structure and Argument

The comparison between the "Photocopier Test," a neural network's average voice, and room resonances provides a strong, unified theory of attractors in audio processing.

The "What the Attractor Tells You" section clearly delineates the different types of attractors (harmonic, spectral, perceptual, statistical). You establish that degradation is not simply loss of information, but rather the extraction of the medium's inherent characteristics.

The "Plasticity Gap" section, however, is a detour. While the deepfake detector's neuroplasticity is interesting, it dilutes the central theme of iterative processing and attractors. Deepfakes are single-generation artifacts, not the result of iteration. The connection to "attractor signatures" at different scales is tenuous and distracts from the elegance of the Lucier/I2D parallel.

## Clarity and Flow

The concept of convergence rate as a measure of channel richness is explained beautifully. You effectively demonstrate why a better TTS model degrades more slowly (it carries more information) while a white noise generator converges instantly (it has no memory).

## Style and Voice

The tone is insightful, authoritative, and successfully bridges the gap between DSP engineering and experimental composition.
