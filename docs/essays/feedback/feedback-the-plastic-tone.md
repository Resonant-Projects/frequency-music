# Feedback: The Plastic Tone
## Overall Impression

The essay contains a promising distinction between pitch height and “pitch authority,” and it draws a musically fertile connection among spectral manipulation, perceptual salience, and identity under transformation. Its central term, however, never stabilizes. “Plastic” refers alternately to developmental change in birdsong, studio malleability, perceptual ambiguity, and the capacity to survive transformation. Those meanings are not equivalent. The argument would be more defensible if plasticity meant a tone’s capacity for controlled transformation while retaining a specified perceptual invariant; the birdsong finding could then be treated as a limited empirical analogy rather than the source of a universal principle.

## Structure and Argument

The essay proceeds source by source, with the “Study” section integrating them only near the end. That structure causes repetition with related essays: pitch strength, kinds of inharmonicity, birdsong, and multipitch complex tones each receive a miniature summary, but the causal relation among them remains unclear. Reorganize around three questions: what remains invariant, what can be manipulated, and how would a listener know identity survived?

The birdsong inference is the largest logical gap. Lower spectral flatness is not synonymous with “more tonal and structured” in every relevant sense, and correlation with a latent-space trajectory measure does not show that tonal structure is a scaffold for learning. The phrases “most open to developmental change” and “most learnable voice” overgeneralize from a specific species, representation, and analysis. Report the actual scope and frame the compositional lesson as an analogy.

The multipitch section also needs perceptual evidence. A monophonic waveform can contain harmonics corresponding to several pitch interpretations, but “one source at the waveform level” is imprecise: sourcehood is not a waveform property, and harmonic spectra routinely contain pitch-class-related components without yielding separate lines. What matters is whether listeners segregate or track those components.

The study could anchor the essay, but it currently combines noise, inharmonic offsets, and time-varying spectral emphasis without defining an invariant. Specify what remains constant—dominant pitch judgment, source identity, or note label—and acknowledge that these may diverge. The ending should answer its own question with a bounded claim rather than imply that impurity inherently increases expressive capacity.

## Clarity and Flow

“Tonality,” “tone,” “pitch strength,” “pitch authority,” and “pitch identity” are used as though interchangeable. Tonality usually concerns a system of pitch relations, not the pitchedness of one sound. Replace the opening’s “tonality” with “pitch salience” unless the essay genuinely addresses tonal hierarchy. Define spectral flatness and clarify that lower values indicate a more peaky spectrum, not automatically a single clear pitch.

The “AI music systems” paragraph leaps from an analytical descriptor to a controllable generative parameter. A model might estimate pitch strength without independently changing it while holding register and loudness fixed. Mark these as design goals requiring validation.

## Style and Voice

The essay’s voice is strongest when it asks “where is pitch authority located now?” That phrase unifies attention, spectrum, and compositional agency without pretending to be a standard scientific metric. Consider making “pitch authority” the essay’s main compositional term and reserving “plasticity” for transformation capacity.

Several lyrical claims—“the tone were learning what kind of voice it wants,” “noisy envelope that still remembers the note”—are appealing but personify the material at points where a concrete production instruction would help more. Use them sparingly as conclusions to technical passages.

## Line-Level Edits

- “tonality is not simply present or absent in a sound” should be “the salience of a pitch percept can vary continuously.”
- “made more plastic by the developmental path that produced it” needs a precise referent and evidence; the cited correlation does not establish causation by developmental path.
- “how strongly a note declares itself as a pitch” is elegant but circular. Add: “how reliably and saliently listeners perceive a pitch from the sound.”
- “how much of the sound should be allowed to count as a tone” implies a quantitative partition the concept does not provide. Try: “how strongly the sound should function as pitched material.”
- “interactions among discrete partials” should identify whether the measure captures detuning, beating, multiple sources, or deviation from harmonic templates.
- “recent popular music may remain more inharmonic” needs dates, corpus limits, effect magnitude, and citation; “recent” is undefined.
- “trajectory variance estimates how much a vocalization would change” should specify that this is a model-derived measure, not direct longitudinal observation if that is the study design.
- “This is not the same as writing a chord” needs a criterion: source count, perceptual fusion, or notation alone does not distinguish the cases.
- “keep the sound tonal” should become “preserve a stable dominant-pitch judgment,” which is testable.
- “what does it become more capable of expressing because it is not perfectly pure?” romanticizes impurity. Consider: “Which transformations preserve its identity, and which produce a perceptibly different object?”
