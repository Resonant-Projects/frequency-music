# Feedback: The Teaching Window

## Overall Impression

The essay proposes a compelling term for the timescale and context over which musical evidence becomes learnable or confirmable. Its strongest contribution is the claim that a window embeds assumptions about identity, rather than merely sampling neutral data. Yet “teaching” remains underspecified. Sometimes it means model training, sometimes online inference, sometimes a listener’s perceptual integration, and sometimes compositional emphasis. These processes operate through different mechanisms and timescales. Unless the essay distinguishes them, the central metaphor risks concealing rather than clarifying the argument.

## Structure and Argument

The progression from definition to examples, compositional control, study, and interface is sound. The opening’s dependence on three internally named concepts—“Decision rate,” “The layer that answers,” and “The accessible next state”—makes the essay feel like a node in a private conceptual graph rather than a standalone argument. Briefly define each or link it at first mention.

The hierarchy “transient teaches attack, beat teaches groove, bar teaches motif, section teaches form” is intuitive but overly schematic. Groove can require multiple bars; source identity can depend on sustained resonances; form can be cued in seconds; affect can shift at an onset. Present these as experimental scales, not fixed mappings. More importantly, a window has more dimensions than duration: causal versus centered access, overlap, hop size, spectral resolution, prior context, annotations, and task all determine what can be learned. Since the definition promises “time, resolution, and context,” the rest of the essay should actually analyze those axes.

The proposed listener study is not yet a valid test of the teaching-window concept. The five transformations differ in content as well as window, so any grouping difference would be confounded by the cues retained. Specify matched manipulations, participant task, counterbalancing, and what outcome would falsify the hypothesis.

## Clarity and Flow

“The teaching window decides which facts get enough repetition to become evidence” is too narrow: evidence can accumulate without repetition, and some cues are diagnostic in a single event. “Accessibility Has Memory” also shifts from reachable state spaces to learned continuity without explaining the causal bridge. State directly that earlier representation choices constrain what later transitions can preserve; do not call that memory unless the system retains history.

The source note is too vague to support empirical claims. “Recent durable graph syntheses” and “extraction clusters” cannot be independently checked. Provide paper-level references for critical-band perception, watermarking, equivariance, and score understanding, and mark the teaching-window synthesis as the author’s proposal.

## Style and Voice

The pedagogical voice suits the subject, and the final sentence has genuine resonance. The prose nevertheless relies too often on personification: a representation “teaches,” a window “decides,” a piece “gives a fact” time. Retain the metaphor, but alternate it with literal descriptions of what a model receives, what a listener integrates, and what a composer controls. Capitalize headings consistently: “The Window Becomes a Teacher,” not “A Teacher,” unless title case is a house style applied everywhere.

## Line-Level Edits

- “It learns what counts from the window it is given” → “What it can learn or infer depends on the temporal and contextual evidence it receives.” This is more precise while preserving the premise.
- “A teaching window is the span of time, resolution, and context” → “A teaching window is the bounded evidence—duration, temporal resolution, surrounding context, and task labels—available for learning or confirming an identity.”
- “If it hears a beat, it learns groove” → “A beat-scale window may expose local timing cues, while longer windows may be needed to establish recurring groove.”
- “A phase-sensitive representation may expose relational timing” should specify which phase relation and cite evidence; phase sensitivity by itself does not guarantee musically useful timing information.
- “Critical-band orchestration teaches the ear in perceptual bands” → “Critical-band orchestration allocates energy according to one model of auditory frequency resolution.” The original makes an unsubstantiated learning claim.
- “The point is to decide where identity gets educated” → “The point is to decide over which evidence span a particular identity becomes inferable.”
- “The interesting result would be disagreement” → “The hypothesis is that category judgments will dissociate across transformations; preregister which dissociations the window account predicts.” Disagreement alone could reflect ambiguity or poor stimuli.
- “They are different curricula” → “They impose different evidence regimes.” This is less lyrical but far more exact; the curriculum metaphor can remain elsewhere.
