# Feedback: Pitch Is a Negotiation

## Overall Impression

A strong, conceptually coherent essay that reframes pitch from a static property into a dynamic perceptual settlement. The three-source structure (inharmonicity history, pitch strength, harmonic complex tones) builds a genuine argument rather than a survey. The producer's version grounds it in practice. This is one of the more successful essays in the collection—it has a clear thesis, evidence that maps to the thesis, and a concrete exercise.

## Structure and Argument

The three sections correspond to three distinct pressures on pitch: spectral (inharmonicity), perceptual (strength), and structural (multipitch from single tones). This is a good decomposition.

The inharmonicity section makes a crucial distinction: "noise is not the same problem as a crowded partial field." This is the essay's sharpest analytical move. It prevents the "inharmonicity" label from becoming a wastebasket.

The pitch-strength section correctly identifies this as an independent compositional parameter. The bullet points (increase salience for orientation, reduce for texture, rise before cadence) are practical and specific. This is where the essay earns its keep for a composer.

The harmonic-complex-tone section is the most provocative: "a single tone can contain more than one path." The reframe from "what pitch is this?" to "which part of the spectrum is the listener invited to follow?" is excellent—it shifts agency from the signal to the compositional decision.

The "Deeper Claim" (pitch as a vote, a perceptual settlement) synthesizes well. The final line—"Pitch is not only something to choose. It is something to stage."—is a strong closer.

## Clarity and Flow

The essay moves cleanly from theory → perception → composition. The historical frame (1961–present inharmonicity) gives the first section temporal weight. The transition to pitch strength as "the missing middle term" is well-signposted.

One flow issue: the "Producer's Version" section feels slightly disconnected from the three-source structure. It reads as a fourth section rather than an integration. A bridging sentence—"These three pressures converge in the studio as..."—would stitch it tighter.

## Style and Voice

Confident, direct, composer-facing. "Pitch is not a point. It is a vote." — excellent compression. The tone respects the reader's intelligence without assuming specialized knowledge.

Crutch watch: "This is not X. It is Y." appears four times. It's a useful rhetorical move but overused here. Also "The important move is..." and "The useful bridge..." — vary the transition phrases.

## Line-Level Edits

- "The ear can still find pitch inside many of these textures." → "The ear still finds pitch in many of these textures." (active)
- "Sometimes the pitch becomes weaker. Sometimes it becomes more ambiguous. Sometimes it becomes richer..." → Three "sometimes" sentences in a row. Combine: "The pitch may weaken, ambiguate, or enrich..."
- "If pitch strength varies inside a song, then it can shape form." → Strong conditional. Keep.
- "The notated pitch may stay fixed while the perceptual pitch hierarchy changes." → Good. Consider: "Notation fixes the pitch; perception rewrites the hierarchy."
- Exercise section: "Do not change the nominal note. Change only the distribution of spectral evidence." → Crisp. Keep.
