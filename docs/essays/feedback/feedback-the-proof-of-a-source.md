# Feedback: The Proof Of A Source
## Overall Impression

This essay has a compelling compositional premise: a piece can organize not only source material but the cues by which source identity becomes available over time. The witness/correlation/deadline sequence gives that premise a strong narrative shape, and the “proof budgets” are a practical synthesis. The core sentence—“A source is whatever the listening system can prove in time”—is nevertheless too reductive. Sources may exist without being segregated or recognized, different systems may infer different source structures, and “proof” here is a decision under uncertainty rather than logical demonstration. The essay should distinguish physical source, perceptual auditory object, dataset label, and model target.

## Structure and Argument

The opening repeats much of “The Proof Burden of Sound” and relies on the same proof-complexity analogy. To justify a separate essay, foreground what is unique here: sourcehood as an outcome of evidence accumulation, not the general cost of all musical interpretation. The first section should define whose source is at issue—physical cause, heard stream, or classified event—and introduce the time-bounded evidence metaphor with an explicit limit.

“The Clean Event As Witness” is inventive, but the description of FSD50K-Solo needs exactness. Synthetic single-class examples are training references, not witnesses proving that a messy real recording has one cause. State the curation pipeline, labels, filtering threshold, and validation procedure. Also avoid equating “single class” with “single source”: one dog barking repeatedly, several dogs, or a dog plus room reflections complicate that identity.

“Correlation As Argument” productively translates separation into orchestration, but it shifts from learned time-frequency correlations to human grouping cues without evidence connecting the model architecture to perception. Cite auditory-scene research for the musical claims, or clearly mark the transition as analogy. The list of strong, weak, and false proof is useful but risks treating cue agreement as sufficient; common fate can bind multiple physical sources, while one source can generate conflicting cues.

The streaming section is more about linguistic commitment than sourcehood. It supports the deadline component but not source proof directly. Tighten it and spend the saved space on revision: how later evidence re-segments or reattributes an auditory stream. The etude then becomes the persuasive culmination. The conclusion should say that composers can shape the conditions of attribution, not literally write a proof.

## Clarity and Flow

Define “effective source knowledge,” “proof strength,” and each budget in terms of possible observations. “Memory budget,” for example, is not interchangeable with capacity unless you specify duration or recognition performance. “Revision budget” is particularly promising but needs explanation: is it the degree to which retrospective reinterpretation is permitted by the material?

The essay repeatedly uses legal/logical vocabulary—witness, argument, proof, false proof—without distinguishing metaphor from method. One explicit paragraph establishing the mapping would reduce caveats elsewhere: reference example as prior evidence, correlated cues as support, threshold crossing as provisional judgment, later cues as revision.

## Style and Voice

The voice is assured and musical, especially in the clean-to-buried compositional example. Preserve that specificity. Some aphorisms become categorical where the more interesting claim is conditional. “Recognition Is A Proof With A Deadline” works as a title-like provocation; in the body, prefer calibrated verbs such as “supports,” “licenses,” and “makes available.”

There is also redundancy in the final three sections: each restates bounded evidence and delayed action. Compress “Effective Source Knowledge” so the proof-budget list and study have more room to specify actual listening outcomes.

## Line-Level Edits

- “before a system can act on sound, it has to prove what kind of sound it is hearing” should be “many systems must estimate a task-relevant category or structure before acting.”
- “That word, ‘prove,’ is not only metaphorical” is misleading because none of the audio systems supplies formal proof. Try: “The metaphor is useful because both domains confront bounded evidence and deadlines, though their standards differ.”
- “FSD50K-Solo constructs proof of singleness” should be “operationalizes a criterion for likely single-source examples.”
- “A source is whatever…” should become “For a bounded listener or model, usable source identity is whatever can be supported strongly enough, soon enough, for the task.”
- “clean single-class events” should not be treated as synonymous with physically clean single-source recordings without the dataset’s definition.
- “The first event becomes evidence for the second” is strong; add that recognition must be tested rather than assumed.
- “correlation is how a line argues for its own identity” needs “in this compositional analogy” to avoid conflating model features and perception.
- “False proof” should be “misattribution” or “illusory grouping”; a strong cue does not constitute proof.
- “The body entrains now” is evocative but universalizing. Try: “Sensorimotor prediction and entrainment often operate before a phrase is complete.”
- “what cannot be proven in time may as well be unavailable” should specify “unavailable for the immediate task”; late evidence can still reshape memory and interpretation.
