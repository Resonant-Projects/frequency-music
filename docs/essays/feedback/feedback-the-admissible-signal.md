# Feedback: The Admissible Signal

## Overall Impression

This essay develops a strong thesis: every musical AI system defines the evidence path by which sound can be classified, segmented, or controlled. The concept of "admissible signal" is novel and important—it reframes technical benchmark design as a compositional question. The essay connects Echoes, music structure analysis, spatial audio, room acoustics, and frequency representation bottlenecks into a coherent concept.

## Structure and Argument

The essay's five-domain structure builds a compelling case:
1. **Echoes**: Semantic alignment restricts what evidence counts
2. **Music Structure Analysis**: Annotation conventions shape admissible evidence
3. **Spatial Audio**: Source vs. room factor admissibility
4. **Room Acoustics**: Input availability determines valid predictions
5. **Frequency Bottlenecks**: Representational basis shapes admissible signal

The progression from dataset design → representation → encoding is well-ordered. The "admissible signal" concept emerges naturally.

The three design questions at the end are excellent compositional prompts. The experiment (five versions of the same material, one evidence channel each) is concrete and testable.

## Clarity and Flow

Clear and well-written. The synthesis paragraph—"When those rules change, the same audio becomes a different kind of evidence"—is the thesis statement.

## Style and Voice

Analytical and precise. The voice doesn't overclaim.

## Line-Level Edits

- The essay could benefit from more specific musical examples in the compositional applications section.
- The connection to "the right to interpret" could be explored more.
