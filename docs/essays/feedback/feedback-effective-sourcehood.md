# Feedback — "Effective Sourcehood"

## Overall Impression
The third essay in what is effectively an "effective X" trilogy. This one is the most philosophically committed of the three and the most compact. Its central move — distinguishing ambiguity from complexity — is genuinely useful and doesn't appear as clearly in the sibling essays. What weakens the piece is that it never confronts the fact that it's covering territory the trilogy has already covered.

## Structure / Argument
- The essay is efficient — five sections, each earning its place.
- The cryptographic framing ("if a vulnerability exists but cannot be proven, then it cannot be exploited") is the strongest analogy in the trilogy and deserves more weight. Consider leading with it.
- The room-impulse-response paragraph is a real addition — reverberation as evidence-of-place that weakens evidence-of-source is a compositionally rich observation. This idea is buried; promote it.
- "Ambiguity means several interpretations remain open. Complexity means the correct interpretation may exist, but the listener would need an impractical amount of evidence to establish it." — this is the essay's original contribution and should be closer to the opening, not the closing.
- The five-step studio experiment is well-shaped but reads as a variation on the ones in the sibling essays. Sharpen the difference.

## Clarity / Flow
- Compact and readable.
- "The hidden source is not enough. It has to leave a proof the ear can afford." — strong closer.
- The pivot from cryptography to audio at "Audio has an analogue" is clean.

## Style / Voice
- Consistent with the series.
- The essay uses "operational" and "effective" heavily; this is characteristic of the trilogy but starts to feel like a tic.
- Fewer rhetorical flourishes than the audibility essay, and the piece is better for it.

## Line-Level Notes
- "But practical systems do not operate on metaphysical causes. They operate on evidence that can be extracted, grouped, labeled, and acted on." — good.
- "The dataset has to manufacture conditions under which the source can be proven well enough for learning." — sharp; the word "manufacture" is doing real work.
- "reverberation supplies evidence of place, scale, and material, but it also spreads the source through time" — one of the best sentences in the trilogy. Build the essay around it.
- Consider consolidating the three "effective" essays into one, or explicitly labeling them as parts I / II / III.
