# Feedback: The Resolution Budget

## Overall Impression

The essay develops a productive compositional principle: fidelity should be allocated according to task rather than maximized indiscriminately. Its concrete studio recipe gives the concept practical force, and the distinction between intelligibility and reconstruction is especially clear. The main editorial problem is scope. “Resolution” comes to mean temporal sampling, spectral selectivity, bit allocation, cross-stream alignment, harmonic specificity, and path dependence. “Budget” implies a finite resource and a tradeoff, but several examples merely involve selecting an analysis scale, not reallocating a constrained capacity. Define the resource being budgeted in each domain—or narrow the essay to representational precision under capacity constraints.

The source claims need citations with enough information to evaluate them. Give exact frequency ranges, tasks, metrics, and paper links for rhythm formants, ClariCodec, Qwen3.5-Omni, and WST-X. The ice and Tonnetz material is analogical rather than evidentiary and should be marked as such.

## Structure and Argument

The essay’s sections create a clear widening arc from rhythm through codecs, alignment, microdetail, harmony, and procedure. The “Harmony” section is where the argument strains. A Tonnetz’s graph structure and Ostwald’s step rule do not demonstrate a resolution budget; they suggest constrained traversal and metastability. Either develop a precise mapping—what finite representational resource is allocated, what is sacrificed, and what remains exact—or remove this section so the essay’s technical core stays coherent.

“Alignment fidelity” is a useful secondary concept, but it is not obviously resolution. Timing precision between layers can be discussed as one budget dimension if the essay explicitly says that resolution includes tolerance in relational alignment. Without that, the term expands opportunistically.

The studio recipe is testable in spirit but not controlled enough for its claimed lesson. Version A’s rhythm manipulations, Version B’s articulation, and Version C’s added texture differ in salience and likely loudness beyond integrated matching. Define measurable targets, use multiple listeners, randomize order, and ask task-specific questions rather than only “which layer announces itself first?” The latter measures attention, not whether the music “stays itself.”

The conclusion usefully returns to selective preservation, but the six-source recap repeats the opening. Compress it and state the boundary: a budget is meaningful only relative to a task, constraint, and failure criterion.

## Clarity and Flow

The opening contrast is clear, yet “fidelity,” “resolution,” “precision,” “detail,” and “intelligibility” drift into one another. Define them separately. Fidelity is similarity to a reference; resolution is discriminable granularity; intelligibility is task performance; precision may be temporal, spectral, or symbolic. The essay depends on these distinctions.

“Rhythm formants” requires a definition and cautious wording, since readers may assume an exact analogue to vocal-tract formants. “1–10 Hz modulation” in the recipe is much broader than the opening’s unspecified low-frequency range; cite the choice and explain how the profile will be compared.

## Style and Voice

The voice is strongest in practical formulations such as “what deserves precision?” and “make the path exact, let the destination breathe.” Retain those. Some aphorisms outrun the analysis: “A mix can have pristine stems and poor prosody” transfers a speech term to instrumental coordination without definition. “Fidelity can be aimed instead of sprayed everywhere” is vivid but slightly glib given the engineering complexity.

There is also redundancy with “The Resolution That Counts” and “The Resolution Grid.” Within this essay, emphasize allocation under constraint—the meaning specific to “budget”—rather than the broader claim that tasks require different details.

## Line-Level Edits

- “at 200 bps” should clarify whether this is total bitrate, payload bitrate, or a reported operating point, and cite the exact comparison baseline.
- “improve word error rate” is incorrect idiom because lower WER is better. Use “reduce word error rate.”
- “wavelet scattering … require[s] small temporal averaging” is too absolute. Say the reported detector benefited under the tested datasets and parameter settings.
- “roughly the range we would recognize as pulse…” needs the actual frequency band and evidence connecting linguistic modulation peaks to musical pulse perception.
- “different dialects of the same material” risks turning named languages into a decorative analogy. Consider “distinct rhythmic variants of the same material.”
- “spend resolution on the modulation spectrum first” is an unsupported production prescription. Qualify it: “when rhythmic identity is the target, test prioritizing…”
- “the equivalent of word error rate” should be “a task-specific success measure analogous to WER.”
- “A mix can have pristine stems and poor prosody” could become “Individually clear layers can still have imprecise inter-layer timing.”
- “Ostwald’s step rule says physical systems…” is overgeneralized. Specify the crystallization context and cite a primary or authoritative source.
- “Freeze the chord-node sequence” undermines the later question of harmonic-route identity; explain why harmony is controlled rather than budgeted in this study.
