# Feedback: The Partial Authority
## Overall Impression
“Partial authority” is an original and musically promising term for the varying influence of spectral components on pitch, line, and texture. The essay’s weakness is that “authority” remains metaphorical: it sometimes means perceptual salience, sometimes pitch strength, sometimes independent pitch induction, sometimes structural stability, and sometimes controllability. These properties can diverge. A loud partial may be salient without producing a separate pitch; an absent fundamental can yield strong residue pitch; a stable inharmonic component can shape timbre without acting as a voice.

Define partial authority operationally through one or more measurable listener effects—for example, a component’s influence on pitch matching, stream segregation, melodic tracking, or dissonance judgments. The plural “partials” also needs care when the essay includes broadband noise, which is not ordinarily described as a set of resolved partials.

## Structure and Argument
The sequence from pitch strength to internal counterpoint, inharmonicity/noise, and birdsong development is ambitious. The first three can form a coherent spectral argument; the birdsong case is much weaker. A correlation between trajectory variance and spectral flatness in zebra finch vocalizations does not show that learnable change “requires handles,” nor does lower flatness simply mean “more tonal” without analysis of how flatness was calculated. The essay labels the finding modest, then derives a broad developmental principle anyway. Either remove this section or frame it as a speculative question and report effect size, sample, developmental stage, and alternative explanations.

The harmonic-complex-tone study bears much of the central thesis. “Producers reportedly used” is too indirect. Clarify the task, number and expertise of participants, stimulus construction, response method, and whether listeners independently perceived multiple pitches or producers intentionally composed that interpretation. A monophonic waveform is not necessarily perceived as a single source, and multiple pitch percepts do not automatically constitute “simultaneous melodic lines.”

The studio exercise is the essay’s strongest practical component, but its four states imply a single authority continuum where several parameters change. Add a listening task: ask whether listeners can track a designated line, identify pitch, or hear a second stream. Otherwise the exercise demonstrates timbral variation, not partial authority.

The “small politics” ending is vivid but pushes the metaphor toward intentional agents. Let it close the essay only after authority has been grounded in perceptual consequences.

## Clarity and Flow
“Pitch strength” needs definition and distinction from pitch salience, pitch clarity, tonalness, and confidence in an estimated F0. Cite the measurement method used by the source. Claims that it “helps manage polyphonic dissonance” and that upper harmonics become audible “under conditions of perceptual richness” are opaque and need exact support.

“The fundamental may name the tone” excludes missing-fundamental perception and sounds with ambiguous F0. “Upper harmonics…secondary voices” requires resolved components, sufficient separation, and listener-dependent grouping. Qualify both.

The historical inharmonicity claim needs corpus, feature definition, genre/geographic scope, and direction of change. “Popular music from 1961 to 2020” sounds comprehensive when it likely describes a bounded dataset.

## Style and Voice
The essay’s language is energetic and composer-facing. Phrases such as “a stack of negotiable pitch claims” and “internal counterpoint” effectively name an artistic possibility. The repetition of “authority” in nearly every paragraph, however, sometimes replaces more precise terms. Alternate with salience, grouping influence, pitch evidence, or structural role when those are what you mean.

Avoid “not just timbre” as a dismissive contrast. Timbre perception already involves spectral and temporal organization and can interact with pitch and streaming. The novelty lies in focusing on task-specific influence, not in separating the concept cleanly from timbre.

## Line-Level Edits
- “The fundamental may name the tone” → “A fundamental or inferred F0 may dominate the perceived pitch.”
- “degree to which a component of a spectrum is allowed to behave” → “degree to which a spectral component influences a specified perceptual or musical judgment.”
- “This is not just timbre” → “This reframes one aspect of timbre in terms of task-specific influence.”
- “It has force” → “Listeners may judge its pitch as more or less salient or unambiguous”; name the source’s exact construct.
- “upper partials became musically usable enough to imply simultaneous melodic lines” → report whether this was measured as producer intention, listener perception, or both.
- “A single source can distribute voice-leading” → “A single synthesized signal may support perceived pitch motions associated with different spectral regions.”
- “Broadband noise can reduce pitch authority” → “Adding broadband noise may weaken pitch salience through masking or reduced harmonic evidence”; cite conditions.
- “more tonal and spectrally structured” → avoid equating low spectral flatness with tonality; say “less noise-like under the study’s spectral-flatness measure.”
- “Learnable change may require handles” → “One hypothesis is that stable spectral features support recognizable variation; the cited correlation does not establish this mechanism.”
- “without changing the MIDI pitches” → “while holding MIDI note numbers constant”; rendered F0 or perceived pitch may still shift with synthesis.
