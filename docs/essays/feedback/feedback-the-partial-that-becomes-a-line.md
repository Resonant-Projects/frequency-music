# Feedback: The Partial That Becomes a Line
## Overall Impression

The essay has a strong governing question—when does spectral content begin to function as musical agency?—and the closing image of “other lines hiding” gives that question real compositional charge. Its main weakness is epistemic slippage. It moves too easily among a reported producer practice, psychoacoustic perception, analytical interpretation, and a proposed interface for AI music tools. Those are related but not interchangeable. In particular, the title and several declarative passages imply that an upper partial literally becomes an independent melodic line, while the cited experiment appears to establish only that some producers used or interpreted complex tones as conveying multiple pitches. The essay needs to specify who heard what, under which task and listening conditions, and whether the result was measured perceptually or reported qualitatively.

## Structure and Argument

The progression from pitch strength, to partial-based voice-leading, to a studio study, to a “pitch-agency map” is coherent. However, the opening spends three source summaries before the central synthesis appears. State the discriminating thesis earlier: the essay is not merely claiming that timbre affects pitch, but that partial salience may sometimes support line-like inference without a second fundamental.

The four-quadrant axis is attractive but underdefined. “Pitch strength” and “ambiguity” may not be independent dimensions, and labels such as “tonal atmosphere” and “spectral weather” are poetic classifications rather than testable regimes. Either identify them explicitly as a compositional heuristic or define observable criteria. The “Upper Harmonics As Voice-Leading” section also needs an argumentative bridge: perceptual salience does not itself establish voice-leading. Voice-leading implies temporal continuity, grouping, and relational motion. Explain what evidence would show that listeners track a partial across successive tones rather than merely hear changing brightness.

The proposed study is the essay’s strongest opportunity for precision, but “keep the fundamental” is insufficient control: changing upper-partial amplitudes can change residue pitch, loudness, roughness, and apparent source identity. The ending should acknowledge those confounds and frame the pitch-agency map as a research/design proposal, not the conclusion already demonstrated by the sources.

## Clarity and Flow

The prose is generally clean, but several key terms shift meanings. “Partial” sometimes means a component of one harmonic complex, sometimes a perceptually isolated pitch, and sometimes an automated synthesis control. Define “pitch strength,” “partial salience,” “harmonic fusion,” and “pitch agency” at first use, and distinguish harmonic from inharmonic components. “Upper harmonics becoming audible” is also too loose: harmonics are always acoustically present to varying degrees; the claim concerns perceptual segregation or pitch salience.

The historical paragraph about studio production broadens the scope faster than the evidence warrants. A corpus trend in inharmonicity does not by itself show that production changed “the operating conditions” of pitch perception. Narrow this to an inference and name possible causes rather than assigning agency to “studio production” as a whole.

## Style and Voice

The essay’s compact aphorisms suit its exploratory voice, especially “Do not only ask what pitch a sound has.” Keep that cadence, but reduce the number of metaphors competing to name the same phenomenon: “spectral contract,” “hidden polyphonic interior,” “spectral ensemble,” and “pitch-agency map” currently accumulate faster than they clarify. Select one central metaphor and let the technical distinctions do more work.

Avoid describing an experimental target as “beautiful” before its evidentiary status is secured. The voice will feel more authoritative if wonder follows precision. Capitalization in section titles should also follow a consistent house style (“as” and “a” need not be capitalized).

## Line-Level Edits

- “may involve upper harmonics becoming audible under conditions of perceptual richness” is opaque. Consider: “may change as individual upper harmonics become perceptually salient enough to influence pitch judgments.”
- “If the observation holds under controlled listening” implies that it may not have been tested that way. Replace with a factual description of the study design, or: “That producer-reported use raises a perceptual question that controlled listening would need to test.”
- “a partial is not merely color on a fundamental” overstates the result. Try: “a partial may contribute more than undifferentiated timbral color.”
- “Traditional harmony assumes a relatively stable mapping between sounding body and pitch object” is historically sweeping and conflates harmony with acoustics. Try: “Many notation-centered accounts treat a sound’s pitch as more stable than its spectrum.”
- “several pitch candidates live inside one sound” should become “several pitch interpretations may be cued by one complex sound.”
- “voice-leading can occur between salient partials” needs qualification: “salient partials may support line-like continuity across successive tones.”
- “AI music controls” abruptly narrows the application. Add a sentence explaining why this interface follows from the perceptual argument.
- “Those are the handles” should be “Those could be candidate handles”; the sources do not establish that all five controls are independently estimable or useful.
- “The threshold matters more than the effect” is asserted, not shown. Try: “Mapping the transition threshold would make the effect more reproducible.”
- “Generative models…can accidentally discover” anthropomorphizes the model and speculates about discovery. Replace with: “Model outputs may contain configurations that producers find musically useful before those configurations have a settled analytical vocabulary.”
