# Feedback: The Accessible Phase

## Overall Impression

This essay presents a compelling thesis: composition happens in the "accessible phase," not the ideal space. By connecting ice physics (Ostwald's step rule), Tonnetz geometry, and codec theory, it argues that musical transformation is path-dependent. The concept is valuable—it's one of the stronger theoretical pieces in the collection.

## Structure and Argument

The essay builds its case through three domains:
1. **Ice phases**: metastable states under pressure
2. **Tonnetz geometry**: formal availability vs. realized accessibility
3. **Codec theory**: layered identity under bandwidth pressure

The synthesis—"When transforming material, move to the nearest state that preserves the intended identity layer, not the nearest state in the full parameter space"—is the thesis. It's well-supported.

The proposed layered representation tool (pitch, voice-leading, rhythm, spectral, source, history) is a strong idea, though it could be more specific about how it would work.

## Clarity and Flow

Clear and well-structured. The ice metaphor is the strongest technical analogy because it shows path-dependence concretely.

One flow issue: the essay covers a lot of ground. The jump from ice physics to speech codecs to Tonnetz theory could use more explicit transitions.

## Style and Voice

Analytical and precise. The voice avoids overreach. "Call it the accessible phase principle: under constraint, music becomes the nearest structure that can still carry its identity." — excellent summary.

## Line-Level Edits

- The opening distinction (clean space vs. harsh world) is effective.
- Some transitions between technical examples could be smoother.
- The compositional rule at the end could be more specific and actionable.
