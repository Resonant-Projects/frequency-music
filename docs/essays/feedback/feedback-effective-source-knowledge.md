# Feedback — "Effective Source Knowledge"

## Overall Impression
This essay is close kin to "Effective Audibility" and "Effective Sourcehood" — three pieces working the same territory with slightly different framing. Taken on its own, this is a competent, workmanlike piece. Taken in the context of the series, it raises a real question: is this a distinct concept, or is it the same concept restated? The four-axis breakdown (purity, correlation, latency, proof length) is the piece's earned contribution.

## Structure / Argument
- The FSD50K-Solo / SR-CorrNet / streaming SpeechLLM / effective-zero-knowledge quartet is the same one as in the "Effective Audibility" essay. The overlap is significant enough that the two essays should either be merged or clearly differentiated in their opening moves.
- The four axes are the piece's best contribution. "Proof length" as a distinct axis from "latency" is a subtle and useful distinction and deserves more space.
- The studio experiment is competent but generic — it could plug into any of the "effective X" essays without change. Give this one an experiment specific to source knowledge (e.g., testing which cues fail first as evidence is stripped).
- The closing paragraph reaches for a lyrical register ("someone or something") that the rest of the essay hasn't built toward. Earn it or drop it.

## Clarity / Flow
- "The listener may know enough to hear breath but not instrument, metal but not object, room size but not wall material, human gesture but not linguistic content." — very good; this is the one paragraph that makes the concept concrete.
- The transition from the four sources to the definition happens smoothly.
- "That last axis is the bridge to proof complexity" — good signposting, then well-used.

## Style / Voice
- Consistent with the series. The essay knows its register.
- "Musical phrase functions like a proof whose length can be composed" — sharp.
- Some repetition: "operational," "effective," "practical" cycle through in nearly every paragraph. Vary or commit.

## Line-Level Notes
- "Perfect separation imagines a clean original hiding behind the mixture." — good.
- "which aspects of sourcehood are available enough to govern the next musical decision" — this is the real question of the essay and should be closer to the top.
- "Before that threshold, the sound behaves as material. After it, the sound behaves as someone or something." — pretty, but the "someone or something" flourish is not what the four axes have prepared us for.
- Consider explicitly cross-linking to "Effective Audibility" and "Effective Sourcehood" and stating what each one covers that the others don't.
