# Feedback: The Shape of Musical Choice

## Overall Impression

This essay attempts to map high-level topology onto the act of composing a fugue, but it ultimately fails to bridge the gap between descriptive math and prescriptive composition. The "synthesis" is merely rhetorical, leaving the reader with a set of metaphors rather than actionable insights. The essay overpromises on the utility of Tymoczko's geometric models.

## Structure and Argument

The essay sets up a false dichotomy between "infinite freedom" and "mathematical constraint." No serious composer believes they are choosing from an infinite palette; constraints (style, instrument, genre) are a given. Presenting the idea that "composers explore a specific space" as a revelation is intellectually weak.

The transition from chord orbifolds to fugue statistics is disjointed. The essay leaps from continuous geometric spaces (Tymoczko) to a discrete statistical analysis of Bach's fugue subjects (principal component analysis). These are two entirely different mathematical paradigms. You cannot claim they are part of the same "underlying structure" without explaining how a discrete melody path maps onto the continuous orbifold topology. You treat them as interchangeable "math things" rather than engaging with their structural differences.

## Clarity and Flow

The essay fails to explain _why_ the topology matters. You state that four-note set-class space is a "cone over the real projective plane." Assuming the reader knows what that is (a massive assumption), you never explain what that means for a _composer_. Does the cone shape prevent certain voice leadings? Does the projective plane dictate resolution? Without functional application, this is just dropping vocabulary.

## Style and Voice

The tone is smug, presenting standard music theory (voice leading constraints) wrapped in complex topological jargon as if it were a newly discovered scientific truth. The concluding aphorism ("the deepest patterns... are the shape of the space") is meaningless poetry that attempts to mask the lack of a rigorous conclusion.

## Line-Level Edits

> "The Tonnetz... doesn't fully capture the annular structure of the underlying space. The interscalar interval matrices do better, depicting the crossing-free subgroup of the homotopy group."
> **Critique:** This sentence is pure obfuscation. You are burying the reader in jargon ("annular structure," "interscalar interval matrices," "crossing-free subgroup") without defining a single term or explaining its musical equivalent. If you cannot explain the "crossing-free subgroup" in terms of actual notes moving on a piano, delete the sentence.

> "The first principal component captures 51% of the variance; three components get you to 80%."
> **Critique:** Variance of _what_? You mention six dimensions earlier, but listing percentages without specifying which features are driving that variance makes the statistic useless. Is the variance driven by pitch range? Rhythmic density? If you don't interpret the data, you aren't doing analysis; you're just quoting a paper.
