# Feedback: Perceptual Resolution

## Overall Impression

This is a sharp, focused essay that identifies a genuine perceptual control knob—grain size of distinction—and demonstrates it operating across four distinct domains. The concept of "perceptual resolution" is well-chosen: it reframes texture from a static surface quality into an active parameter with a threshold. The piece works because it resists the temptation to make the ML papers do all the work; the compositional study at the end is concrete and immediately actionable.

## Structure and Argument

The four-domain structure (supersaw density, multi-talker attention, four-bar emotion tracking, time-compressed speech) is effective because each domain maps to a different axis of musical perception: harmonic identity, stream segregation, affective contour, prosodic continuity. This isn't just a list—it's a proof that the concept generalizes.

One structural weakness: the ZipL-Dialog example (25 Hz latent) and the four-bar emotion window are both *temporal* compression, while supersaw density and multi-talker attention are *simultaneous* fusion/segregation. The essay treats them as equivalent "grain" choices, but they operate on different dimensions. A sentence distinguishing *temporal resolution* from *simultaneous resolution* would sharpen the taxonomy.

The "Compositional Implications" section correctly identifies that the threshold itself is the material. Moving music across the boundary where recovery fails is a stronger compositional strategy than picking a resolution and staying there.

## Clarity and Flow

The opening question—"how much detail can disappear before the musical object changes identity?"—is excellent. It frames the entire piece as an investigation of identity conditions rather than signal processing.

The transition from Cook's supersaw to the ML papers is smooth. The phrase "These are not the same domain, but they share a shape" is a good signpost.

The final numbered list (pick identity → decide resolution → move across threshold → make threshold audible) is a clean procedural summary. It could be tighter: step 4 ("make the threshold itself audible") is the actual compositional payoff; steps 1-3 are setup. Consider leading with the payoff.

## Style and Voice

The voice is precise and analytical without being academic in the deadening sense. Phrases like "perceptual resolution names the control knob underneath it" and "the beautiful part is that those limits are not merely obstacles" show good editorial instinct—making the abstract feel tactile.

One crutch pattern: "X chooses whether Y" appears three times in the domain list. Vary the verb. Also "not merely X but Y" appears twice in close proximity.
