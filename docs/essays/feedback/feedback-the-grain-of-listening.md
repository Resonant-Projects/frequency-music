# Feedback: The Grain of Listening

**Overall Impression**  
This is a sprawling, ambitious essay that tackles a massive philosophical question: why does perception turn continuous reality into discrete events? By pulling evidence from TTS prosody metrics, plant biology, and neural audio codecs, you build a compelling case that discretization is a fundamental property of how physical systems interact with the world. 

**Structure and Argument**  
The three examples in the opening section ("Three Counters") are excellent and diverse. They immediately establish that you aren't just talking about human hearing, but a broader principle. 

The argument flows well from the *fact* of discretization to the *reason* for it ("Why Discrete?") and finally to the implications for musical structure and composition. 

The section "The Exponential Decay Problem" is brilliant. Connecting the technical problem of Residual Vector Quantization to Schenkerian analysis is the kind of cross-disciplinary insight that makes these essays so strong.

**Clarity and Flow**  
The writing is generally very strong. You do a good job of explaining technical concepts (like edit distance and RVQ) without bogging down the philosophical argument.

However, the paragraph about plants in "Three Counters" feels slightly under-explained. You say they "track the number of discrete events... a form of rudimentary numerical cognition." Adding one half-sentence explaining *what* kind of events they are tracking (e.g., "counting the number of times a pollinator touches a leaf" or whatever the actual biological mechanism is) would make this abstract point much more visceral.

**Style and Voice**  
The tone is appropriately grand but grounded in technical reality.
