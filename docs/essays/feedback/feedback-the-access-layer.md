# Feedback: The Access Layer

## Overall Impression

This essay delivers a crucial distinction: fidelity versus steerability in audio models. The core insight—"a compressed representation can sound convincing while hiding the handles a composer would want to touch"—is immediately valuable for anyone working with ML-generated audio. The neural network access layer concept is novel and compositionally potent.

## Structure and Argument

The essay makes four strong points:
1. Structural bottlenecks in end-to-end models (strided convolutions creating aliasing)
2. Bodily access (infrasound at hearing/vibration boundary)
3. Clinical access (respiratory traces in voice)
4. Evaluation access (rank-ordering without calibration)

Each point is well-supported by sources and builds toward the central principle: "A musical system is defined not only by what it contains, but by which distinctions remain accessible at the point of action."

One structural note: the clinical and evaluation sections feel slightly rushed compared to the detailed first two. They could benefit from equal depth.

## Clarity and Flow

The opening paragraph is exceptionally clear—it immediately establishes the fidelity/steerability trade-off that defines the entire essay. The shift from "neural encoders are imperfect" to the sharper claim about strided convolutions is effective.

The essay moves logically from model access → bodily access → clinical access → evaluation access. This progression from technical to biological to applied feels natural.

The "Composition can use this deliberately" section with its bullet questions (Which distinctions preserved for performer? For listener? Etc.) is excellent—it turns analysis into compositional leverage.

## Style and Voice

Analytical but accessible. The voice avoids excessive technical jargon while remaining precise. Phrases like "wager about cochlear, vestibular, tactile, speaker, and room access" make the abstract tangible.

Crutch watch: "That distinction matters musically" and "Here the representation pressure is..." appear in similar forms elsewhere. Consider varying these transitions.

## Line-Level Edits

- "The model may reconstruct plausible audio while losing controllable access to the primitives that make pitch and timbre separable. Fidelity survives; steerability does not." → This is the essay's thesis statement. It's perfect. Keep.
- "A sub-bass line is therefore not a single musical object distributed identically to all listeners. It is a wager about cochlear, vestibular, tactile, speaker, and room access." → This is vivid. Consider moving it to open the bodily access section earlier.
- The bullet list of questions (Which distinctions should be preserved...?) is strong. Each question could start with a verb for parallelism: Preserve, Collapse, Hide, Reappear.
- Final sentence: "The hidden layer under all of this is not representation. It is access." → Excellent closer.
