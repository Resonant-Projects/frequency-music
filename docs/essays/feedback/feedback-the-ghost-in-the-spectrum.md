# Feedback: The Ghost in the Spectrum: Why We Hear Frequencies That Aren't There

## Overall Impression

This essay is a masterful exploration of the "missing fundamental" illusion. It successfully bridges the gap between the raw physics of sound (Fourier analysis) and the cognitive algorithms of the brain (pattern matching/periodicity extraction). The explanation is clear, the historical context is fascinating, and the compositional takeaways are highly practical.

## Structure and Argument

The structure is flawless. You introduce the phenomenon (listening to bass on a small speaker), explain the biological paradox (the frequency isn't in the air or on the basilar membrane), and then solve it using the two competing theories (Pattern Recognition vs. Temporal Fine Structure).

The section "The Brain as a Comb Filter" is the highlight of the piece. Explaining the brain's pitch-extraction process as a temporal auto-correlation mechanism (looking for the lowest common denominator of the time intervals between neural spikes) is an incredibly elegant way to explain a highly complex neurobiological process. It avoids the trap of treating the brain like a simple spectrum analyzer.

The "Why Did We Evolve This?" section grounds the neurobiology in evolutionary reality (extracting a single speaker's voice from a noisy background). This provides a necessary "why" to accompany the dense mathematical "how."

## Clarity and Flow

The explanation of why Tartini tones (difference tones) are NOT the missing fundamental is crucial. Many texts confuse the two. Your clarification that Tartini tones are physically present in the cochlea due to nonlinear distortion, while the missing fundamental is purely a central nervous system computation, is excellent and scientifically rigorous.

## Style and Voice

The tone is inquisitive, clear, and highly engaging.

"The fundamental isn't a frequency you hear; it's a conclusion your brain reaches." This is a spectacular summary sentence. It perfectly encapsulates the entire essay in one easily memorable phrase.
