# Feedback: Sourcehood Before Signal

## Overall Impression

A concise, focused essay that makes a sharp architectural claim: source separation needs to happen early in the pipeline, not late. The connection to composition—treating "source-legibility" as a controllable parameter—is the essay's main value. It succeeds in translating a technical insight into a musical tool.

## Structure and Argument

Three sources, one central claim:
1. **SR-CorrNet**: early separation prevents information bottleneck
2. **FSD50K-Solo**: sourcehood as supervision precondition  
3. **Anomalous sound detection**: identity as hidden variable

The synthesis—"A sound is not just a spectrum evolving over time. It is evidence for a body"—is the essay's thesis and it lands well.

The five practical axes (Single-source clarity → Source hallucination) are clear and compositionally useful. The sketch at the end (cross-assigning reverbs, spectral masking, resolving to processing chain) is a concrete proposal that could be implemented.

## Clarity and Flow

Concise and well-paced. The essay moves quickly from technical claims to compositional implications without lingering. The "For composition, this suggests..." paragraph is an effective pivot.

The sketch could be tighter—steps 1-5 are clear but the musical rationale for each step could be more explicit. Why cross-assign reverbs? To destabilize the identity/space alignment. Say that.

## Style and Voice

Direct and analytical. The tone is confident without overclaiming. "This is more than a mixing trick. It is a way to compose with the listener's inference engine." — strong positioning.
