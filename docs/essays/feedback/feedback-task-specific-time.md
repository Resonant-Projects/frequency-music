# Feedback: Task-Specific Time

## Overall Impression

An important conceptual essay that resolves the confusion around "audio understanding" at one universal clock rate. The core claim—that time is task-dependent rather than monolithic—is well-argued and offers real compositional value. The evidence across music-games, dance generation, ReGen, and SPAM is diverse enough to justify the thesis without overgeneralizing.

## Structure and Argument

The essay builds its case on four distinct temporal clocks:
1. Beat-relative time (music-game level generation)
2. Section-scale intention vs. motion-frame time (Wan-Dancer)
3. Decision rate vs. rendering rate (ReGen/ReGenVoice)
4. Articulatory feature time (SPAM)

Each clock gets a well-crafted paragraph. The musical consequence is the essay's strongest argument: each clock opens a different compositional control lane.

One structural strength: the "practical design test" near the end. Instead of just abstract argument, it gives the reader something concrete to try. That moves the essay beyond theory into Frequency's instrument-building spirit.

The thesis statement—"A system understands sound at the rate demanded by the task"—is memorable and frames the entire piece.

## Clarity and Flow

The essay flows well from music-games to dance to generation to speech. The transitions are handled with signal phrases that connect each domain.

One flow improvement: the ReGen/ReGenVoice section could be tightened. The sentence about 12.5 Hz and 6.25 Hz latents could be more concise.

## Style and Voice

Clear and analytical, with a proper humility toward the limits of generalization. The tone respects the reader's intelligence. "None of these clocks is more real than the others" is a strong, balanced claim.

Crutch watch: "The musical claim is plain" appears twice in close proximity. Vary.
