# Feedback: The Source Identity Budget

## Overall Impression

“Budget” could be a valuable addition to the collection because it implies a finite resource and a tradeoff. At present, however, the essay does not establish what is finite, who spends it, or how source ambiguity competes with harmony, rhythm, form, or text. The paper summaries support source dependence and domain shift, not an attentional budget. The proposed tool then converts the metaphor into a measurable curve without specifying a target construct or ground truth. Revision should either operationalize the budget or rename it as source-continuity confidence.

## Structure and Argument

The question “How much of a sound can change before the listener loses track of what made it?” is excellent and more precise than the bold thesis that follows. Build the essay around that psychophysical question. Each source could contribute a class of perturbation—mixture, contamination, withheld identity, domain shift—but the current summaries do not report thresholds or listener tracking. Make explicit that these machine-learning results motivate, rather than answer, the perceptual question.

“Source identity is…part of the signal itself” is conceptually unstable. Identity is inferred from signal cues and context; it is not literally contained in the waveform as a label. The essay later says identity consumes attention, which introduces a different mechanism. Cite auditory attention or object-continuity research if the budget is meant cognitively, and distinguish attentional demand from acoustic evidence.

The infant-cry section is the least integrated. Dataset shift within the class “cry” shows variability, but does not necessarily concern source identity; shifts may arise from recording equipment, labeling, clinical population, or preprocessing. Report the datasets, cross-domain performance, and evidence that F0/MFCC/STFT effects are infant-dependent. Otherwise, use this as a caution about class labels hiding domains, not as direct support for source identity.

The “Preserve / Spend / Exploit” triad is memorable and should organize the second half. Yet “spend identity” suggests an irreversible finite quantity when the cues can be restored. “Modulate identity evidence” may be technically sounder. The tool proposal must define its output: continuity probability between event pairs, source-label posterior, or human-rated perceptual continuity. A single curve cannot capture competing source assignments; a graph or posterior over bindings may be more appropriate.

The final project-brand sentence is generic and dilutes the ending. Conclude by answering the opening question: not with a universal quantity, but with a proposed experimental method for finding cue-dependent thresholds.

## Clarity and Flow

“Instrument identity, performer identity, room identity, and gesture identity” are not interchangeable. A listener can track one performer through an instrument change or one gesture across performers. Define the identity level the essay concerns, then acknowledge nested identities.

“Voice-leading is…continuous preservation of source identity” is an evocative analogy, not a definition. Voice-leading can deliberately transfer a line between instruments, and perceptual streams do not map cleanly to notated voices. Qualify the statement and use it as a compositional example rather than evidence.

## Style and Voice

The prose is concise, confident, and sensibly sectioned. Its main stylistic liability is making metaphors carry empirical claims: identity “consumes attention,” papers “add pressure,” notation “reopens” richness, and a confidence surface becomes “measurable” by declaration. Keep the budget metaphor, but repeatedly identify where it is heuristic and where measurement is proposed.

The repeated formula “is not X; it is Y” creates artificial certainty. More conditional phrasing would suit an essay centered on ambiguity.

## Line-Level Edits

- “source identity is not a label attached after listening. It is part of the signal itself” → “source identity is inferred during listening from signal cues, context, and prior expectations.”
- “all consume part of the listener’s attention” → “may compete for attention under dense or ambiguous conditions,” with a citation.
- “Voice-leading is…continuous preservation of source identity” → “One function of orchestration and voice-leading can be to preserve a stream across overlapping events.”
- “The ear hears one source because those components cohere.” → “Listeners often group those components as one event because several temporal, spectral, and causal cues cohere.”
- “A wrong note on a flute…” confounds note identity, instrument, and location. Explain exactly which judgment changes.
- “source identity behaves like a budget because it competes” needs supporting evidence or should read “The budget metaphor proposes that resolving attribution can compete…”
- “estimate a source identity budget” → “estimate time-varying evidence for continuity between candidate source streams.”
- “The result would be a measurable way” → “The result could be tested against listener judgments of continuity.”
