# Feedback: Symmetry as the Skeleton of Music

## Overall Impression

This essay attempts a grand synthesis of Lie algebras, crystallographic reflection groups, and gauge theory, but it collapses under its own weight. The essay throws profound-sounding mathematical frameworks at music theory without ever actually demonstrating _how_ they compute a musical result. It is an exercise in metaphor masquerading as mathematics.

## Structure and Argument

The essay treats three entirely different mathematical frameworks (Tymoczko’s orbifolds, Noll’s Tonnetz, and the Gauge Theory of tonal attraction) as if they are part of a single unified theory, but it fails to do the hard work of actually unifying them.

The "Gauge Theory" section is particularly problematic. The essay claims that "tonal attraction is a mathematical necessity derived from gauge symmetry breaking." This is a massive claim that is asserted without proof. How exactly does a broken gauge symmetry calculate the resolution of a V7 chord to an I chord? The essay waves its hands at "static attraction profiles" without providing a single concrete example of the math in action. If you cannot walk the reader through the calculation for a simple cadence, the framework is just an elaborate metaphor.

The "Langlands Duality" paragraph is pure jargon soup. Stating that "swapping long and short sides of the fundamental alcove transforms an all-major tonnetz into an all-minor one" sounds impressive, but without defining what those sides represent physically or acoustically, it is incomprehensible to both a musician and a mathematician.

## Clarity and Flow

The essay is choked with terminology that is never adequately unpacked. Phrases like "Hilbert spaces," "G₂ tonnetz," and "crystallographic reflection groups" are deployed rapidly to create an aura of rigor, but they actively prevent the reader from understanding the underlying mechanics of the argument.

## Style and Voice

The tone is arrogant and dismissive of traditional music theory ("not just a metaphor," "the skeleton that music is built on"). When making claims this grandiose, the burden of proof is exceptionally high, and this essay fails to meet it. It reads like a physics undergraduate who has just discovered music theory and believes they can "solve" it.

## Line-Level Edits

> "The G₂ tonnetz, for example, produces only 12 of the 24 major and minor triads, arranged with circle-of-fifths relationships and diminished seventh chords at the vertices."
> **Critique:** This is presented as a profound constraint, but _which_ 12 triads? If it doesn't map to a musically useful subset (like a specific key or a known symmetrical scale), then it's just a mathematical curiosity, not the "skeleton of music." You must ground these abstract geometries in audible musical results.

> "The gauge model gestures at dynamics, but it's really about static attraction profiles..."
> **Critique:** If it only provides static profiles, then it fails completely as a model of music, which is fundamentally a temporal, dynamic art form. You brush past this limitation as if it's a minor detail, when in fact it invalidates the entire premise of the essay. A "skeleton" that cannot move is a fossil, not a living system.
