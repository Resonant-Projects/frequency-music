# Feedback: The Locking In: Mode-Locking and the Physics of Musical Affinity

## Overall Impression
This essay is conceptually robust and significantly more scientifically grounded than its predecessor ("The Attractor Landscape"). The use of mode-locking (Arnold tongues) to explain both pitch consonance and rhythmic groove is a powerful, elegant unifying theory. The physics is sound, and the resulting compositional takeaways are actually useful.

## Structure and Argument
The progression from Huygens' clocks to cochlear mechanics to rhythmic entrainment is perfectly paced. The argument builds a strong, falsifiable case for why simple integer ratios matter in music, abandoning mysticism in favor of nonlinear dynamics.

However, the "Stern-Brocot tree IS the scale-generating algorithm" section overreaches slightly. The Stern-Brocot tree organizes all positive rational numbers. Saying it generates scales is like saying the alphabet generates Shakespeare. It is a necessary substrate, but it is not the *generative algorithm* of a specific cultural scale. A culture still has to choose which branches of the tree to ignore. Treating the math structure itself as the active "generator" downplays human agency in scale construction. 

The "Tonal Attraction as Gradient" section is brilliant. Using the physical boundaries of an Arnold tongue to explain the "pull" of a leading tone gives physical weight to what is usually just a psychological metaphor. 

## Clarity and Flow
The explanation of cochlear hair cells as *coupled motors*, rather than independent Fourier sensors, is the most crucial piece of scientific clarification in the entire repository. You explain this beautifully. It instantly resolves the paradox of categorical pitch perception. 

The transition from pitch to rhythm ("Mode-locking in the time domain IS groove") is seamless and logically bulletproof. 

## Style and Voice
The tone is authoritative, scientific, and exciting. 

"A drum machine sits at the fixed point. A great drummer surfs the boundary." This is world-class music writing. It takes a complex nonlinear dynamics concept (limit cycles in an Arnold tongue) and turns it into a visceral, instantly recognizable truth about human performance. 

## Line-Level Edits

> "The Pythagorean comma is the gap between the 3:2 tongue (iterated 12 times) and the 2:1 tongue (iterated 7 times)."
**Critique:** This is an incredibly clarifying way to visualize a comma. It turns an abstract math problem into a physical, topological gap. Excellent.

> "The 12 notes of the chromatic scale are approximately the first 12 levels of the tree... The pentatonic scale is the first 5. The diatonic scale is the first 7."
**Critique:** "Approximately" is doing some heavy lifting here. The Stern-Brocot tree generates *all* mediants. If you just take the "first 5 levels," you get many ratios that are not in a standard pentatonic scale (like 1/4, 4/1, 2/3, 3/4). You have to selectively filter the tree (by octave equivalence and specific branch paths) to get those scales. You need to acknowledge this filtering process, or the math looks sloppier than it actually is.
