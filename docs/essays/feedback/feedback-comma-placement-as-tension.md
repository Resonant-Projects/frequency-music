# Feedback: Comma Placement as Tension

## Overall Impression

This is a strong conceptual essay built around an elegant formulation: temperament does not remove tuning discrepancy; it distributes it. The metaphor of debt, geography, corridors, and local rooms gives composers a useful way to hear tuning systems as formal choices. Its largest problem is technical overcompression. “The comma” shifts among the syntonic comma, the Pythagorean comma, generic beating, and any tempered interval discrepancy. Those are related but not interchangeable. Because the thesis depends on precision, the essay should name which comma quarter-comma meantone tempers out, how equal temperament closes the circle of fifths, and why the proposed progression does or does not expose those differences.

The central listening experiment is attractive but presently under-specified and in places internally inconsistent. A defensible revision would retain the voice while turning the recipe into a genuinely controlled comparison.

## Structure and Argument

The essay progresses cleanly from thesis to local/global tradeoff, meantone geography, an experiment, compositional use, and conclusion. The weak link is the experimental progression. At 72 BPM in common time, 24 bars last 80 seconds only if each chord receives six beats, not six bars; six four-beat bars per chord yield 120 seconds. More importantly, I–vi–IV–V in C does not seriously test remote-key navigation or the wolf intervals used to motivate meantone geography. It tests diatonic sonorities around one tonic.

The just-intonation condition is ambiguous: “relative to each local chord where possible” describes adaptive chord-by-chord retuning, not a single fixed 5-limit scale. With a fixed C drone, locally pure roots and thirds may introduce pitch drift or contradictory tuning obligations. State whether common tones keep a fixed frequency, whether every chord root is derived from C, and what happens to the syntonic comma between alternative derivations of D or A. Otherwise listeners are comparing an unspecified algorithm to two fixed temperaments.

The expected ordering—JI calmest, meantone intermediate, 12-TET most tense—should be posed as a hypothesis, not a “straightforward” result. Roughness depends on register, spectrum, voicing, level, duration, vibrato, and listener experience; equal temperament does not guarantee a globally perceived increase in tension. The ending can still claim that discrepancy is compositional material, but should not imply one universal affective ranking.

## Clarity and Flow

Define “comma” on first use and distinguish tuning error from audible beating. A comma is a small intervallic discrepancy produced by incompatible tuning constructions; beating is one possible acoustic consequence when sounding partials are close. “Simple frequency ratios align partials cleanly” also needs qualification: this is most direct for harmonic complex tones, and sensory consonance is not exhausted by partial coincidence.

The essay repeats the local-purity/global-mobility contrast in nearly every section. Keep the clearest formulation in “Consonance Is Local; Modulation Is Historical,” then let later sections add mechanics or compositional consequences rather than restating it. The “Aha” mostly summarizes prior claims; it would be stronger if it named the experiment’s possible disconfirmation and narrowed the conclusion accordingly.

## Style and Voice

The aphoristic voice suits the topic, and “where should the music put its unavoidable disagreement?” is an excellent governing question. Some metaphors subtly moralize the systems: equal temperament’s “persistent grayness,” just intonation’s “truth,” and meantone’s “hidden debt” predispose the listener before the blind test. Preserve the geography but make its valence less fixed. “Sweetness” and “radiance” can be attributed to historically situated listening descriptions rather than presented as acoustic facts.

## Line-Level Edits

- “The expected order is straightforward…” Replace with: “One testable hypothesis is that, for sustained harmonic tones in this register, listeners will rate the JI render as least rough and the 12-TET render as most beating-rich.”
- “The comma is not eliminated.” Clarify: “Temperaments handle different comma discrepancies differently: quarter-comma meantone tempers out the syntonic comma, while 12-TET distributes the Pythagorean closure error across twelve fifths.”
- “A pure fifth at 3:2 and a pure major third at 5:4…are acoustic agreements.” Consider: “…produce exact partial coincidences for ideal harmonic tones; their perceptual effect still depends on spectrum and context.”
- “The composer is choosing between local acoustic truth and historical navigability.” Replace “truth” with “purity” or “fit”; “truth” makes one tuning metaphysically privileged.
- “Equal temperament offers…a persistent grayness in the thirds.” Try: “Equal temperament offers uniform transposability while tempering every major third by the same amount.”
- “Write a 24-bar progression at 72 BPM…six bars per chord” conflicts with “80-second.” Correct the duration or meter.
- “using the ordinary fixed semitone grid” could be “using twelve equal logarithmic divisions of the octave,” which is both concise and exact.
