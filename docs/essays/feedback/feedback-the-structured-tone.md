# Feedback: The Structured Tone
## Overall Impression

The essay proposes an appealing producer-facing concept: tonal organization can be redistributed among a fundamental, harmonic or inharmonic partials, noise, and change over time. Its practical study is strong. The scientific synthesis, however, overstates what the four sources jointly show. Pitch strength, historical inharmonicity descriptors, perceived polyphony from harmonic complexes, and a correlational birdsong-plasticity measure do not establish one latent property called “structured tone.” The proposed term could still be valuable as a compositional construct, but it should be introduced as a framework built from distinct dimensions, not as a stronger empirical concept discovered across the papers.

## Structure and Argument

The essay’s trajectory—from definition, to partials as voices, to types of inharmonicity, to plasticity, to interface—is coherent. The birdsong section is the weakest link. A reported association between trajectory variance and lower spectral flatness does not justify “Plasticity Prefers Structure,” “Learnability may … belong,” or claims about what sounds are most transformable. Correlation may reflect species, developmental stage, recording conditions, vocalization type, or model bias. Give the correlation magnitude and controls, define trajectory variance, and restrict the inference to the measured dataset.

The internal-polyphony section also needs perceptual detail. Were listeners actually tested, were multiple melodies intentionally encoded and identified, or did authors make an analytic observation? “Two or more perceived pitch paths” and “the listener can hear internal counterpoint” need the study design, sample size, task, and outcome. A monophonic production stream is not necessarily one physical or perceptual source, and prominent resolved partials do not automatically function as independent voices.

The “structured-tone control” currently combines five non-ordered categories. Fundamental energy, harmonic salience, discrete inharmonicity, periodic noise, and trajectory are axes or carrier locations, not positions on one scalar degree of organization. Recast the interface as a multidimensional surface. Then the study’s three controls become the logical culmination rather than contradicting the earlier singular “control.”

The ending is elegant but circular: defining tone as structure does not tell readers how much organization is required. End with an operational proposal—listeners’ ability to make stable pitch, stream, or transformation judgments under controlled manipulations.

## Clarity and Flow

Key terms need definition: pitch strength, pitch salience, tonality, tonal center, harmonicity, inharmonicity, noisiness, spectral flatness, and “pitch authority.” The essay often treats them as interchangeable. Pitch strength is usually a perceptual attribute, spectral flatness a signal descriptor, and harmonicity a relation among components; each can dissociate from the others.

The historical claims are broad. “Popular music moving through long historical phases” and production techniques “increase inharmonic complexity” need corpus dates, measurement method, effect sizes, and controls for mastering, genre, and recording technology. The examples are plausible mechanisms, not demonstrated explanations unless the paper tested them.

## Style and Voice

The producer-oriented voice is confident and concrete, particularly “do not add another voice; change whether a partial is allowed to behave like a voice.” Preserve that line after qualifying “voice.” The prose relies on short aphorisms to jump over evidence: “This is not magic,” “That is a beautiful constraint,” and “A structured tone has handles.” Replace one or two with precise transitions that separate finding from interpretation.

“Tone Is A Degree Of Organization” should use lowercase “a” in title case, as should other section headings, unless this capitalization is a house style.

## Line-Level Edits

- “contains enough organized spectral evidence for pitch to act” → “supports sufficiently stable pitch judgments for a specified musical task.” Define the task and listener population.
- “may make upper harmonics musically audible” → explain what “musically audible” means—detection, attention, melody identification, or analytic relevance—and cite the result.
- “heard as carrying multiple simultaneous melodic lines” → report who heard them and under what task; otherwise use “designed or analyzed as implying multiple pitch trajectories.”
- “polyphony is not only a count of sources” → “perceived polyphony need not map one-to-one onto production streams.” Polyphony is not conventionally defined as source count alone.
- “Noise can weaken pitch authority” → “Increasing aperiodic energy can reduce pitch salience under some spectral and level conditions.” Add a psychoacoustic citation.
- “interactions among discrete partials” → clarify whether the paper means detuned partials, beating, combination tones, or an inharmonicity descriptor; partials do not become inharmonic merely by interacting.
- “The studio … manufactures new degrees of tonal organization” → “Studio processes can alter harmonicity, noisiness, and the perceptual salience of spectral components.”
- “the reported correlation” → provide coefficient, uncertainty, sample composition, and whether lower flatness independently predicts trajectory variance.
- “Pitch-strength concentration. Move authority between the fundamental and upper partials.” → “Spectral pitch-cue distribution. Vary fundamental energy and resolved upper-partial salience independently.”
- “At the edge, tonal structure becomes a memory” → specify the manipulation: “At the edge, current pitch cues weaken enough that continuity depends on preceding context.”
