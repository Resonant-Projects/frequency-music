# Feedback: The Accessible Trajectory

## Overall Impression

This essay extends the "accessible phase" concept into a dynamic framework focused on paths rather than states. It connects ice physics, rhythm formants, prosody alignment, and Tonnetz geometry to argue that accessible trajectories are more important than accessible states. The thesis—"a structure is defined by the path that can actually get it there"—is strong and well-supported.

## Structure and Argument

The essay builds its case through four domains:
1. **Ice phases**: path-dependent state selection
2. **Rhythm formants**: low-frequency modulation bands as accessible handles
3. **Prosody alignment**: dynamic token synchronization
4. **Tonnetz geometry**: formal maps don't specify walkable paths

The progression from static states → dynamic paths is well-executed. The rhythm-formant section (treating rhythm as frequency-domain object) is the essay's most original contribution.

## Clarity and Flow

Clear and well-structured. The opening question ("The Best Form Is Not Always Reachable") is effective. Transitions between sections are generally smooth, though the prosody alignment section could be more directly connected to the trajectory theme.

## Style and Voice

Analytical and precise. The voice avoids overreach. The closing synthesis is strong.

## Line-Level Edits

- The rhythm-formant section is underutilized—it deserves more development.
- Some transitions could be more explicit.
- The compositional rule at the end is solid but could be more specific.
