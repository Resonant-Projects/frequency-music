# Feedback — "Manufactured Clarity"

## Overall Impression
The essay's central reframe — clarity is a procedure, not a condition — is strong and defensible. The problem is that the essay is now the sixth or seventh in the series to draw on the same source cluster (FSD50K-Solo, SR-CorrNet, streaming SpeechLLM, ASR evaluation, binaural rendering, proof complexity). The reader following the series is being served the same cluster with a new label. The essay would be sharper if it explicitly acknowledged this and staked out what it uniquely adds.

## Structure / Argument
- "The clean signal is not the starting condition. It is the result of a procedure." — thesis, well-positioned.
- "Metrics Also Manufacture Clarity" is the most useful section. The critique of task-specific metrics (chord recognizer clear about labels, source separator clear about stems) is precise and generalizable. Extend this.
- "Spatial And Temporal Clarity" opens a genuinely productive idea — that clarity has coordinates and the coordinates can be pulled apart — and then rushes to the next section. This deserves more space.
- The proof-complexity section is thin here because the analogy has already been made in "Effective Audibility." Cross-link or trim.
- The studio exercise is the strongest yet in the series because it makes the trade-off explicit: each clarity procedure sacrifices something. Keep this move.

## Clarity / Flow
- Well-shaped, though slightly overlong for the ground it covers.
- "The metric does not merely report clarity. It defines which clarity the system is allowed to care about." — this is the essay's best sentence and its actual thesis. Move it up.

## Style / Voice
- Consistent.
- "Manufactured" as the operative word is doing more work than "effective" did in the earlier essays; it carries a slight critique that the essay never fully cashes out. Is manufactured clarity a good thing, a suspect thing, or a neutral thing? The essay slides between all three.

## Line-Level Notes
- "A pitch tracker may be clear about F0 while flattening the instability that made the performance alive." — one of the best sentences in the batch.
- "The trade is the material." — good closer, though it belongs at the end, not mid-sentence in the studio-exercise section.
- "Ask what each version had to sacrifice in order to become clear." — this is the essay's operative move; it should also be in the thesis paragraph.
- Consider whether "clarity" is really the right umbrella term or whether "legibility" or "resolvability" would carry the argument more precisely.
