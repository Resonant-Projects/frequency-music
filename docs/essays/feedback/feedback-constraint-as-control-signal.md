# Feedback — "Constraint as Control Signal"

## Overall Impression
The essay's central move — reframing constraint from decorative boundary to control signal — is genuinely useful, and the five-parameter breakdown (scope, duration, permeability, audibility, agency effect) is the strongest thing in the piece. But the essay openly admits its sources are thin ("Several rows are explicitly thin"), and rather than treating that as a warning it treats it as license to spin analogies. The result is a piece that's more suggestive than argued.

## Structure / Argument
- The opening move — "the thinness itself leaves one useful cross-source thread exposed" — is a rhetorical dodge. If the sources are thin, you have to work harder to earn the connection, not less. As written, the essay treats mole-rat pheromones, Li Shangyin's zither, and AI narratives as parallel evidence for a single claim, but they aren't. They're four unrelated things loosely stapled to a metaphor.
- The Li Shangyin section is the weakest. You concede "the excerpt does not prove anything about sound" and then use it anyway as your load-bearing example. Either find a real acoustic source for the poetic-form-as-constraint argument or drop the poem.
- The mole-rat analogy is strained. Chemical suppression of fertility is not compositionally analogous to a drone suppressing harmonic wandering — one is coercive biology, the other is perceptual masking. Calling both "inhibitors" flattens them.
- The five parameters land well but arrive suddenly, and only one (a "queen signal") gets operationalized in the studio experiment. The other four are asserted, not tested.

## Clarity / Flow
- "The Yu Deng extraction gives the cleanest form of the idea" — "extraction" is opaque jargon from the source pipeline; a general reader has no idea what an extraction is.
- The pivot from mathematics (Tsimerman, Wang/Zahl) to "constrained agency" to composition happens fast and the bridge is a metaphor ("proof burden"). The move deserves more argumentative weight.
- The final line — "It tells the ear what counts as motion, what counts as refusal, and what counts as escape" — is beautiful but rhetorical; nothing in the essay has established "refusal" or "escape" as musical categories.

## Style / Voice
- Voice is consistent and generally good — measured, essayistic, willing to hedge ("worth keeping provisional but useful"). The hedging is a virtue here.
- "Signal" is doing a lot of work in this essay: control signal, released signal, queen signal, recurring timbre. The word starts to blur. Tighten.
- The bolded takeaway sentence is a good habit, but this one is oddly abstract for a piece that's been reaching for concrete examples.

## Line-Level Notes
- "Without the tuning frame, the bent pitch is just another frequency." — this is a strong sentence. More like this.
- "It does not merely announce hierarchy; it enforces it chemically." — good, but again, chemistry ≠ acoustics, and the essay never confronts the disanalogy.
- "the harder the constraint, the more audible the solution becomes when it succeeds" — needs qualification. Some hard constraints just fail invisibly.
- The sources section with the opaque IDs remains a problem across the series. If these are internal graph refs, hide them; if they're citations, format them.
