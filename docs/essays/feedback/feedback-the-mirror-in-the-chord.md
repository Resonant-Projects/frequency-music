## Structure and Argument
The structure is flawless. The progression from the "trivial" physics fact (frequency vs. period) to a profound harmonic truth (major vs. minor) hooks the reader instantly. 

The argument that emotional valence might be geometrically encoded (the perpendicular component predicting melancholy) is the most explosive idea in the essay. The way you arrive at it—through physics, then math, then neurobiology—makes a highly speculative psychological claim feel completely inevitable.

The section on seventh chords, specifically highlighting the *Tristan* chord, acts as a perfect real-world anchor. It proves that this isn't just a whiteboard exercise; it describes the actual history of musical innovation.

But earlier, you established that suspended sits *on* the diagonal, while major and minor sit on either side. Does "projecting onto the diagonal" mean calculating their distance *along* the diagonal from the origin, or their distance *to* the diagonal?
