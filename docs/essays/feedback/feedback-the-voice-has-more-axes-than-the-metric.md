# Feedback: The Voice Has More Axes Than the Metric

**Overall Impression**  
This essay makes a vital and highly practical point about audio AI and musical composition: we are asking single metrics to do too much work, collapsing distinct perceptual dimensions (identity, accent, prosody, timing) into unhelpful averages. The synthesis of the four papers is effective and leads directly to actionable advice for composers working with voice models.

**Structure and Argument**  
The structure is very clean. You introduce the "Hidden Split" (the problem of treating voice as a scalar), walk through four distinct examples of this split (accent vs. identity, phonetic evidence, prosodic grammar, turn-taking), and then derive a practical compositional rule ("separate what the ear separates"). 

The connection between the technical finding in voice cloning (that clones of accented speech preserve identity but change accent) and the musical application (changing vowel color doesn't change the singer's identity) is excellent. It grounds the AI research in traditional musical practice.

**Clarity and Flow**  
The writing is exceptionally clear, continuing the punchy, aphoristic style of the recent essays ("It is not a scalar. It is a stack."). 

**Style and Voice**  
The tone is authoritative, diagnostic, and prescriptive. It reads like a manifesto for a new generation of vocal tools.
