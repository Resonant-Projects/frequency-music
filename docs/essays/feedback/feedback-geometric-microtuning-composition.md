# Feedback: Geometric Microtuning: From Sacred Geometry to Composition

## Overall Impression

This essay attempts to bridge Robert Edward Grant’s geometric numerology with practical music theory, but it suffers from foundational logical flaws. The essay treats arbitrary mathematical conventions (like defining a circle as 360 degrees or setting A=432Hz) as profound natural laws. The resulting framework feels more like post-hoc rationalization than a rigorous compositional tool.

## Structure and Argument

The core argument rests on a flawed premise. The essay states that 12 chromatic notes "correspond precisely" to polygon angles when A=432Hz. This is not a physical property of sound; it is an artifact of human measurement systems (Hertz relies on the definition of a "second," and degrees rely on dividing a circle into 360 arbitrary units). Building a tuning system on this is mathematically trivial, not cosmically significant.

Furthermore, the transition in Part 2 is completely unearned. The essay jumps from deriving cents via `(angle / 360) × 1200` to proposing a scale that suddenly relies on "pure 5:4 major 3rds." You cannot claim to derive a scale purely from polygon geometry and then selectively insert acoustic just-intonation ratios when the geometry produces musically awkward results. Pick one framework and justify it.

## Clarity and Flow

The explanation of the Pythagorean comma as a "gap in the spiral" is vague. You need to explicitly define whether this spiral is geometric (a visual model) or acoustic (stacking fifths). Mixing the metaphors confuses the reader.

## Style and Voice

The tone occasionally slips into uncritical mysticism, despite the concluding claim to the contrary. When describing Grant's theories, maintain a more objective, analytical distance.

## Line-Level Edits

> "Grant's research reveals that when music is tuned to A=432Hz... the 12 chromatic notes correspond precisely to the internal angles of regular polygons."
> **Critique:** "Precisely" is objectively false here, as your own subsequent table shows F at 341.3 Hz and B at 483.3 Hz. Do not claim precision where there are rounding discrepancies.

> "If 432Hz = A, then octave-related tempos maintain resonance... Using 108 BPM or 72 BPM (432 ÷ 6) maintains geometric coherence..."
> **Critique:** Dividing frequency (cycles per second) by 6 to get beats per minute (beats per 60 seconds) relies on unit confusion. A 432Hz wave completes 432 cycles in one second. A 72 BPM tempo is 1.2 beats per second. The math here is entirely dependent on the arbitrary 60-second minute, which has nothing to do with acoustic resonance.
