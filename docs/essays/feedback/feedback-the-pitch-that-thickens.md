# Feedback: The Pitch That Thickens
## Overall Impression

“Pitch thickness” is an intuitive production metaphor, and the essay usefully insists that pitch and timbre are not opposites. Yet the proposed parameter currently bundles several partly independent perceptual dimensions: pitch strength, number of pitch percepts, spectral complexity, inharmonicity, roughness, detune density, and line-like affordance. A single low-to-high knob cannot safely represent all of these. Noise can weaken pitch without adding a secondary line; a harmonic complex can have rich spectral structure and a very strong single pitch; two resolved pitches may not sound “thicker” than one noisy one. The essay should present pitch thickness as a multidimensional design space or narrow it to one measurable relation.

## Structure and Argument

The opening summarizes four sources clearly, but the birdsong study does not support the same construct as the other three. A correlation between developmental plasticity and lower spectral flatness in zebra-finch vocalizations does not demonstrate pitch thickness, nor that structured spectra are “available for learning.” It may supply a provocative analogy, but it should be marked as such and accompanied by the study’s sample, unit of analysis, effect size, and alternative explanations.

The “Middle Between Tone And Noise” section correctly distinguishes broadband noise from discrete partial misalignment, but then treats that distinction as an ordered continuum. Consider replacing the thickness ladder with two axes: strength of a dominant pitch center and degree/type of internally resolvable spectral structure. This would preserve the compositional insight while accommodating sounds that are pitch-strong and spectrally rich, or pitch-weak but not internally polyphonic.

The studio experiment is constructive, but “keep the fundamental, loudness, and envelope constant” is not enough to isolate thickness. Specify equal-loudness matching, spectral centroid, modulation, listening level, playback system, and whether “secondary pitches” are free responses or forced choices. If an analysis model is used, it cannot substitute for listener reports unless its validity for these judgments is established.

The final section overstates applicability to generative controls. A useful interface would likely expose separate parameters rather than one “pitch-thickness control.” Let the conclusion argue for preserving and manipulating internal pitch evidence, not for a prematurely unified scalar.

## Clarity and Flow

The definition—“retains a pitch center while accumulating internal spectral routes”—is evocative but not operational. “Route,” “addressable structure,” “bundle,” and “handles” all need translation into phenomena a listener or model can detect. Define whether a pitch center requires stable fundamental-frequency judgment, and whether thickness concerns simultaneous percepts or temporal changes.

The essay also alternates among acoustic properties, perceptual results, and compositional uses without signaling the layer. Inharmonicity is a spectral relationship; pitch strength and roughness are perceptions or estimates; “latent melodic affordance” is an interpretation. Explicit labels would improve causal clarity.

## Style and Voice

The opening and closing are memorable, but repeated crowd/structure metaphors sometimes obscure rather than deepen the concept. The restrained caution—“That does not mean every listener hears stable polyphony”—is exactly the tone needed throughout. Apply the same caution to historical trends and birdsong.

Avoid presenting the project’s internal graph as evidence. Connections to other concepts can orient a series, but they do not validate “pitch thickness.” The essay should earn the concept through discriminations and predicted listening outcomes.

## Line-Level Edits

- “Pitch is not a yes-or-no property” confuses pitch existence with pitch salience. Try: “The salience and internal organization of pitch are not all-or-nothing.”
- “studio production has made popular music persistently more inharmonic” asserts causation. Use: “the corpus analysis reports higher inharmonicity in contemporary popular music and proposes production practices as possible contributors.”
- “birdsong…finds that more developmentally plastic vocalizations tend to have lower spectral flatness” should specify species, analysis method, and correlational status immediately.
- “A thin pitch points cleanly to one fundamental” excludes missing-fundamental pitch. Replace “fundamental” with “dominant pitch percept.”
- “discrete partials interacting” is vague; partials can be individually inharmonic, beat, or arise from multiple sources. Name the acoustic condition described by the paper.
- “pitch strength gives that zone a possible control surface” should be “pitch-strength estimates might help describe one axis of that zone.”
- “The sounds most available for learning” is unsupported. Try: “The correlation raises—but does not answer—the question of whether spectral regularity relates to developmental change.”
- “At high thickness, pitch starts to become texture” conflicts with the definition requiring a retained center. State where the concept ceases to apply.
- “Ask listeners or an analysis model” should distinguish subjective evaluation from automated proxy measures.
- “changing how many internal perceptual routes the note offers” could become “changing the balance among a dominant pitch, secondary pitch cues, roughness, and noise.”
