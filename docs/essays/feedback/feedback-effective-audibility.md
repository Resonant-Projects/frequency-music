# Feedback — "Effective Audibility"

## Overall Impression
Strong central concept, clearly named, useful compositional payoff. The proof-complexity analogy is the load-bearing move and it mostly works. What weakens the piece is that the four-part audibility scale (explicitly / eventually / effectively inaudible / operationally absent) is introduced and then only lightly used; the essay doesn't fully commit to it as a framework.

## Structure / Argument
- The opening is efficient. The move from proof complexity to listening happens fast and lands.
- The SR-CorrNet / FSD50K-Solo / streaming-SpeechLLM triangulation is well-handled — each source contributes a different pressure (architectural, corpus, temporal), and together they earn the concept.
- The four-part scale is the essay's best contribution but is introduced late and never revisited in the studio-test section. The four studio versions correspond loosely to the four categories, but you never explicitly map them. Do it — it would tighten the piece considerably.
- The final section restates the core idea rather than pushing it further. Consider ending on the fourth studio test rather than the recap.

## Clarity / Flow
- "Effective audibility is the condition where a musical or acoustic relation is not merely present, but provable enough to guide listening." — clean, quotable, load-bearing. Good.
- "Sometimes a structure is meant to be heard as such. Sometimes it is meant to pressure the surface without becoming nameable. Sometimes it is a private scaffold that shapes decisions but never asks for recognition." — the strongest passage in the essay.
- The proof-complexity source is described in one sentence and then repeatedly leaned on. Given how much work the analogy does, spend another sentence establishing it.

## Style / Voice
- Consistent. The essay is written with confidence and restraint.
- "The proof of the source has to arrive in time" is a good sentence for the same reason it's a good idea.
- Slightly overreliant on the rhetorical device of ending sections with a single-sentence gnomic pronouncement. Vary it.

## Line-Level Notes
- "A truth that cannot be established within the available budget does not function like usable knowledge." — sharp.
- "The point is to compose the proof conditions." — this is your thesis; consider putting it earlier.
- "one that cannot be proven at all from the sounding surface" — worth asking whether such a version is compositionally interesting or just an inert control condition.
- The connections list at the bottom is well-formatted (using the .md links). This should be the model for the rest of the series.
