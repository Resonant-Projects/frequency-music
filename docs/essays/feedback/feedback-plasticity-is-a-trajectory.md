# Feedback: Plasticity Is a Trajectory

## Overall Impression

An ambitious essay that adds a genuinely new parameter—trajectory plasticity—to the Frequency vocabulary. The birdsong paper is an unlikely but productive source; mapping "trajectory variance" to developmental mobility of a gesture is the kind of lateral move that elevates this from summary to synthesis. The essay has one clear core idea and it defends it well, but the piece is short relative to its scope.

## Structure and Argument

The essay opens with a strong definition: "plasticity as a measurable trajectory: how far a vocal gesture would move if it were produced at another developmental age." This is precise and compositional from the start.

The hinge is sharp: "More plastic vocalizations tend to be more tonal and spectrally structured." This flips the usual noise-to-tone hierarchy—structured spectrum isn't just "cleaner" but is *the thing that can be learned*. That's a genuine insight.

The time-axis layering is the essay's structural claim: beat-relative time, phonological feature time, low-rate latent time, and now developmental time. Listing these four clocks does real work: it shows that time isn't monolithic in music, and each clock opens different control lanes.

The "automate plasticity, not only parameters" suggestion is the compositional payoff. A gesture with a "developmental variance" value is a concrete, actionable concept—exactly what a good compositional parameter should be.

## Clarity and Flow

The essay is dense. Paragraphs run long because each packs a new idea into a paragraph that's already carrying the core definition. The birdsong method is explained concisely but the sentence "The paper reports that this score modestly separates learned song syllables from innate calls in three zebra finches" could be trimmed: "the score modestly separates learned syllables from innate calls."

The transition from the biology to composition is handled well: "That last result is the small hinge." Good signpost.

The final three paragraphs (design test, sourcehood connection, useful concept) feel slightly rushed. The essay rushes toward a conclusion it's earned.

## Style and Voice

Inventive and precise. "Not plasticity as vague flexibility, but plasticity as a measurable trajectory" is a strong opening disambiguation. The voice is analytical but warm—"This is more compositional than it first looks" signals the reader that the payoff is coming.

Crutch watch: "This connects..." and "This also sharpens..." appear in quick succession. Vary.

## Line-Level Edits

- "A note is usually treated as an event with properties... A developing vocalization asks for another property" → "A note is usually treated as an event with properties. A developing vocalization asks for another: developmental mobility."
- "The paper reports that this score modestly separates learned song syllables from innate calls in three zebra finches, and that higher plasticity correlates with lower spectral flatness." → "The score modestly separates learned syllables from innate calls in three zebra finches, with higher plasticity correlating with lower spectral flatness."
- "Some sounds are anchors. Some are probes. Some are still learning their own shape." → This is the essay's best line. Move it earlier or use it as a section closer somewhere other than the final paragraph.
