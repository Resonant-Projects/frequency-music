# Feedback: Sourcehood Is A Commitment

## Overall Impression

This essay reframes sourcehood from a property of the signal to a decision made by the listener/performer/model. The central thesis—"Sourcehood is a commitment"—is strong and memorable. The three-source structure (SR-CorrNet, FSD50K-Solo, streaming SpeechLLM) is the same as the previous two sourcehood essays, but the angle (commitment timing) is distinct enough to justify a separate piece.

## Structure and Argument

The essay's structure mirrors the previous two but with a different lens:
1. **Early Split** (SR-CorrNet): commitment must happen while evidence is rich
2. **Clean Example** (FSD50K-Solo): single-source as curated fiction
3. **Latency Window** (SpeechLLM): action requires commitment before evidence is complete

The compositional exercise (three versions of one material stream) is the strongest part—it gives the reader something concrete to try. The distinction between early commitment (counterpoint), delayed commitment (discovery), and unstable commitment (form) is musically precise.

## Clarity and Flow

The essay is clear and well-paced. The opening question ("when does a sound become one thing?") is effective. The transition to the "Compositional Use" section is smooth.

One flow issue: the "Clean Example" section repeats the FSD50K-Solo summary from the previous essay almost verbatim. If these three essays are meant to be read together, that's fine; if they stand alone, it's redundant. Consider cross-referencing instead of re-summarizing.

## Style and Voice

Analytical and precise. The closing rule—"Do not only decide what the sources are. Decide when the listener has to believe in them."—is an excellent compositional directive.

## Line-Level Edits

- "A source is not simply something that exists in the waveform. It is something a listener, dataset, model, or performer is willing to treat as stable enough to act on." → The thesis. Could be the opening sentence.
- "The more interesting musical question is not 'how many sources are there?' but 'when does the piece force a source decision?'" → Strong reframing. Keep.
- The three versions in the exercise are well-distinguished: early, delayed, unstable commitment. Clear.
- Final paragraph: "The waveform contains possibilities. The piece decides which ones become actors." — excellent closer.
