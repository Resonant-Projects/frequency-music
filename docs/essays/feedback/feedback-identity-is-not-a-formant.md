# Feedback — "Identity Is Not A Formant"

## Overall Impression
One of the stronger essays in the batch. The three-source triangulation (CLP speech, semantic diarization, voice-actor embedding crowding) genuinely earns the central claim, and the "three ways to lose the same voice" structure is doing real analytical work. The compositional proposal is more specific than usual for this series. The essay ends before it can weaken — a virtue.

## Structure / Argument
- The three sources are well-chosen because they attack the same problem from three orthogonal angles (body, scene, style). This is the right kind of triangulation.
- "Vocal identity is not a feature. It is a negotiated constraint between resonance, style, and context." — thesis lands cleanly.
- The three-slider proposal (resonance / performance / contextual identity) is one of the clearer compositional handles produced in the series. It's implementable.
- The essay does not overreach into a grand claim at the end, which is the right instinct.

## Clarity / Flow
- Compact and well-paced.
- The move from ML failure cases to compositional opportunity is done with less friction than usual for this series.
- "Formants matter. Embeddings matter. Semantic continuity matters. Role matters." — sharp rhythm; the essay knows when to be quick.

## Style / Voice
- Consistent, controlled.
- "The most compositionally fertile failure" is the kind of phrase this series does well.

## Line-Level Notes
- "The oboe is the oboe partly because it keeps being the same participant in the texture." — one of the best sentences in the series so far.
- The three-transformation studio exercise is well-scoped. This is the model for what the studio-exercise section should look like across the series.
- "The voice lives where those measurements negotiate with one another." — good closer; the last line "In music, that negotiation can become form" is a slightly weak coda. Consider ending on the "negotiate" line.
