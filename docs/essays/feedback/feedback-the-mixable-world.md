# Feedback: The Mixable World
## Overall Impression
The essay has a useful governing distinction—separation is a technical operation, while mixability is an affordance—but it moves too quickly from three research summaries to a generalized theory of compositional control. “The ontology of the room” is rhetorically effective, yet the piece never decides whether this is a claim about a system’s representation, a user interface, or a listener’s perception. Those are not interchangeable. The strongest version would explicitly argue that these systems render selected components operationally addressable, not that they literally turn a scene into stable stems. This qualification would also strengthen the ending’s emphasis on leakage and partial control.

The source note is not enough for factual auditability. Readers cannot assess reported latency, class counts, clustering labor, or PS4’s losses from internal extraction IDs. Add conventional citations or links, and distinguish paper results from the essay’s extrapolations.

## Structure and Argument
The progression from live class control, to archival clustering, to target-speaker extraction is intelligible, but the hydroacoustic case strains the central analogy. Clustering events into a catalog does not itself make simultaneous ocean layers independently mixable; it makes recorded events searchable or classifiable. The essay acknowledges that the systems differ, then declares that all make “the mixture addressable” without specifying what addressability means across real-time gain control, post hoc indexing, and identity-conditioned extraction. Define a minimal shared operation—perhaps “producing a stable handle for selection or transformation”—and then name the important differences.

The PS4 paragraph arrives after the block quotation and feels appended rather than structurally necessary. Introduce all three systems before stating the synthesis, or organize the body explicitly by class-, event-, and identity-conditioned access. The practical-interface list is valuable, but several verbs are speculative capabilities rather than consequences of the cited work. Mark it as a design proposal and explain what additional tracking or resynthesis each verb would require.

The last three paragraphs are the essay’s best argumentative sequence: promises, partial controls, leakage. They should govern the whole piece. Bring the limits of imperfect separation earlier so the “domesticated world” objection does not appear only after an exuberant set of use cases.

## Clarity and Flow
“Ontology” overstates what is arguably a change in representation and agency. Likewise, “automate attention” may mean automatic gain control, compositional automation, or perceptual guidance. Define it. The phrase “stable layers” needs a criterion: stable across time, recording conditions, class membership, or listener expectations?

The examples are vivid but occasionally outrun the systems. “Move between seasonal marine-mammal patterns and anthropogenic noise” requires reliable semantics, temporal metadata, and possibly manual interpretation beyond unsupervised clusters. “One singer’s consonants” is more granular than target-speaker extraction ordinarily promises. Such examples can remain if framed as prospective tool designs rather than demonstrated functions.

## Style and Voice
The voice is confident, compact, and appropriately musical. Preserve the clean declarative cadence, especially “The new tools are powerful because they move the boundary.” Avoid, however, using aphorism to conceal an unmade distinction. “A scene becomes composable” and “The ocean, too” sound conclusive before the technical bridge has been built.

The two bullet lists provide useful variation, but the second largely restates the preceding synthesis. Consider converting the three-source list into a sentence and keeping the compositional-verbs list, which advances the essay more substantially.

## Line-Level Edits
- “an acoustic scene can become a mix” → “selected components of an acoustic scene can become independently addressable, like channels in a mix.” This preserves the thesis without implying complete decomposition.
- “robust control of up to five overlapping target sounds” → “reported control of up to five overlapping target classes under the paper’s evaluation conditions.” “Robust” needs a named metric and test domain.
- “changes the ontology of the room” → “changes the system’s operational representation of the room.”
- “317 clusters manually mapped to 15 hydroacoustic classes or noise categories in under an hour” needs a citation and clarification of what was timed: labeling clusters, reviewing samples, or the full analysis.
- “Make the mixture addressable before deciding what the mixture means” → “Create handles for the mixture before fixing their interpretation.” This better fits unsupervised clustering, where representation and meaning are not wholly sequential.
- “preserving an identity strongly enough” → “preserving speaker cues strongly enough under the reported overlap conditions.” Specify whether identity preservation was directly evaluated.
- “scan clusters as motifs rather than labels” → “browse clustered events as candidate motifs rather than treating cluster IDs as semantic labels.”
- “hold birdness steady” → “track the intended bird-sound class consistently.” The playful coinage risks making class stability seem simpler than it is.
