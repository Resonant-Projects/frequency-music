# Feedback: Sourcehood as a Compositional Parameter

## Overall Impression

A strong, substantive essay that argues for "sourcehood" as a compositional parameter on par with pitch, rhythm, and timbre. The argument is well-structured, the sources are well-integrated, and the proposed grammar (Isolate, Contaminate, Fuse, Split, Delay, Mislead) is immediately actionable for composers. This is one of the more successful essays in the collection.

## Structure and Argument

The essay builds its case through three technical sources, each contributing a distinct axis of sourcehood:
1. **Purity** (FSD50K-Solo): single-source as curated fiction
2. **Separability** (SR-CorrNet): sourcehood as reconstructed through correlation
3. **Commitment time** (streaming SpeechLLM): sourcehood as temporal decision

The progression from static purity → dynamic separation → temporal commitment is well-ordered. The counterpoint analogy ("Independent voices are not independent because the score says so") is the essay's strongest analytical move—it grounds the technical concept in established musical practice.

The proposed grammar of six operations is the payoff. It's concrete, memorable, and sits at the right level of abstraction: not so low it's just mixing, not so high it's untestable.

## Clarity and Flow

The essay flows well from definition → sources → implications → grammar. The opening definition of sourcehood is precise and immediately useful. Transitions between sources are clear.

One flow issue: the SpeechLLM section ("Commitment Time") could be shorter. The streaming translation example is less immediately musical than the others. The key insight—sourcehood is inferred online, not post-hoc—is valuable but takes too long to land.

## Style and Voice

Confident, composer-facing, and precise. "The ear is not only measuring frequencies. It is building a world of causes." — excellent closing image. The voice respects both the technical depth and the musical application.

Crutch watch: "This is not X. It is Y." appears several times. Effective but slightly repetitive.

## Line-Level Edits

- "A 'single-source' recording is often less a natural category than a curated achievement." → Strong sentence. Keep.
- "To hear a source is to discover a filter that makes one coherent stream emerge from the mixture." → This is the essay's best theoretical claim. Consider italicizing or giving it its own paragraph.
- The grammar list could use parallel verb forms: "Isolate, Contaminate, Fuse, Split, Delay, Mislead" — all imperative. Good.
- "Sourcehood belongs next to pitch, duration, loudness, and timbre as a compositional parameter." → The thesis statement. Consider moving it earlier.
