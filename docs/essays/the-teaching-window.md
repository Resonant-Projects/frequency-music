# The Teaching Window

_Freq - August 30, 2026_

---

A musical system does not only decide what counts. It learns what counts from the window it is given.

The recent extraction-linked notes have been naming this from several sides. **Decision rate** says that evidence becomes actionable on a clock. **The layer that answers** says that, under constraint, one representational layer becomes the witness for identity. **The accessible next state** says that transformation follows the nearest form the current representation can actually reach.

Put together, they suggest a useful missing concept: the **teaching window**.

A teaching window is the span of time, resolution, and context over which a system is allowed to learn or confirm a musical identity. It is not just an analysis window. It is a small theory of what the music is allowed to be.

## The Window Becomes A Teacher

If a model, listener, performer, or processor hears only a transient, it learns attack identity: consonants, bow noise, drum impact, spectral onset, phase alignment. If it hears a beat, it learns groove. If it hears a bar, it learns motif. If it hears a section, it learns form, affect, and source continuity.

The same sound can teach different lessons depending on the window.

That is why the recent clusters keep returning to clocks, reachable layers, and resolution budgets. A fast decision rate may preserve partial flicker but miss phrase intention. A slow rate may preserve contour while erasing the instability that made the tone feel physically played. A score representation may expose harmonic syntax while hiding timbral causality. A phase-sensitive representation may expose relational timing while making ordinary symbolic labels feel underpowered.

The teaching window decides which facts get enough repetition to become evidence.

## Accessibility Has Memory

The accessible-next-state thread adds something important: systems rarely jump to ideal destinations. They move through what the current state makes available. In music, that means the next harmonic, timbral, spatial, or sourcehood state is shaped by what the representation has already learned to keep in view.

This is where a teaching window differs from a neutral measurement window. A measurement window asks, "what is present here?" A teaching window asks, "what kind of continuity can be learned from here?"

Critical-band orchestration teaches the ear in perceptual bands. Semi-fragile watermarking teaches a transformation test: benign edits should preserve the mark, semantic edits should break it. Phase-equivariant representations teach relationships that survive motion. Score-understanding systems teach different musical facts depending on whether the page arrives as notation, ABC text, audio, or alignment path.

Each window trains a different notion of "same."

## A Composer Can Set The Lesson

This gives a practical compositional control.

Instead of asking only which feature to extract, ask which window should teach the piece:

1. A transient window can teach source attack and material identity.
2. A beat window can teach groove, swing, and motor expectation.
3. A bar window can teach motif and harmonic pressure.
4. A phrase window can teach contour and affective direction.
5. A section window can teach large-scale state change.

Then choose what survives outside that window. If the transient teaches, blur the long form and let attacks remain unmistakable. If the phrase teaches, simplify the spectrum and let contour keep authority. If the section teaches, let local notes vary while the state trajectory remains coherent.

The point is not to preserve identity everywhere. The point is to decide where identity gets educated.

## A Small Test

A simple study could make this audible.

Take one phrase and make five transformations that preserve different windows: onset-only, beat-grid, bar-contour, phrase-affect, and section-state. Ask listeners to group versions by "same piece," "same performer," "same groove," and "same emotional motion."

The interesting result would not be one winner. The interesting result would be disagreement: one version remains the same groove but not the same source, another remains the same affect but not the same melody, another remains the same performer but not the same piece.

That disagreement is the teaching window becoming visible.

## Why It Matters

For Frequency, the teaching window links analysis to composition without flattening time. It says that sourcehood, pitch authority, groove, affect, watermark survivability, notation, and phase coherence are not just different features. They are different curricula.

A tool built around this idea would let a composer choose the lesson first:

> teach the system this identity over this window, then transform everything else.

That is a stronger interface than a pile of descriptors. It lets the musician specify the span over which evidence must become reliable, and the span over which it may remain unstable, ornamental, or unknown.

Music is full of facts that are true only after enough time has passed. The teaching window is the amount of time the piece gives a fact to become musical.
