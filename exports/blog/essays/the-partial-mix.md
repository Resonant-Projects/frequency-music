## The Basic Idea

Active noise cancellation tries to erase the world.
Sona tries to edit it.

That difference is everything.

Sona is a real-time, on-device soundscape mediation system that selectively attenuates bothersome sounds while preserving the audio you actually want to hear. It handles multiple overlapping targets, supports new sound classes from in-situ examples, and runs live.

In other words: it does for listening what a good mix does for music.

---

## Total Suppression Is the Wrong Model

The old model of noise control is blunt force. If something is annoying, suppress the whole band, or the whole environment, or the whole channel.

But that destroys context.
It makes the world unnaturally flat.
It solves discomfort by deleting awareness.

Sona starts from a better premise: **perception is selective, not total.**
People do not need all sound removed. They need the right sound made salient and the wrong sound made less salient.

That is exactly what arrangement is.
That is exactly what orchestration is.
That is exactly what mixing is.

---

## A Neural Mix Engineer

Sona's target-conditioned pipeline is a neural version of fader automation. You tell it what category of sound you want attenuated, and it learns to reduce just that source or set of sources.

The important conceptual shift is that the system is not just separating signals. It is **mediating attention**.

That makes it relevant to music in two directions:

1. **Production.** A mix engineer is always deciding what stays foreground, what sinks, and what becomes environmental texture.
2. **Perception.** A listener is always doing the same thing internally, whether or not they realize it.

Sona externalizes that listening act.

---

## User-Defined Categories

One of the most interesting details is that users can define new sound classes from a few examples without retraining.

That is not a minor engineering convenience. It means the ontology of bothersome sound is not fixed.

A concert pianist may define annoyance differently from a subway rider.
A neurodivergent listener may need categories that a generic speech model never anticipated.
A composer may want to suppress only the parts of the room that mask articulation, not the room itself.

This is a subtle but important form of agency: **the listener gets to decide what counts as signal.**

And that is a deeply musical idea.

---

## Why This Matters for Composition

Sona suggests a new kind of compositional tool: one that doesn't just generate sound, but generates *audible conditions*.

Imagine writing a piece where the listener's environment is partially mediated in response to the score:
- speech at the café recedes during an exposed pianissimo
- train noise softens around a rhythmic detail
- a certain class of non-musical sound remains present but no longer masks the gesture

That's not just accessibility. It's compositional staging.
The piece extends into the acoustic ecology around it.

The line between mix and environment becomes porous.

---

## The Deeper Claim

Sona is an argument against all-or-nothing listening.
It says that comfort does not require silence.
It says that awareness and relief can coexist.
It says that a perceptual system can be tuned, not amputated.

That is exactly the kind of claim music makes at its best.
Music doesn't remove the world. It orders attention inside it.

A good arrangement doesn't eliminate complexity.
It teaches you what to hear first.
