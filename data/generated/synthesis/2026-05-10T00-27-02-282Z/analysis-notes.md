## Selected connection: the identity budget

The six selected sources converge on a question of invariance: what survives transformation, what may be discarded, and what should break when identity has changed.

- S1 (Qwen3.5-Omni) and S6 (Balalaika) show prosody as a control layer: token alignment, lexical stress, punctuation, and phoneme annotation preserve the expressive motion of speech rather than only words.
- S2 (ClariCodec) separates intelligibility from acoustic fidelity at 200 bps, making identity an optimization target distinct from reconstruction.
- S4 (StreamMark) formalizes semi-fragile identity: watermarks survive benign transformations but fail under semantics-altering manipulations.
- S3 (Tonnetz geometry) suggests harmonic identity can live in path invariants and adjacency structures, not only named chords.
- S5 (David Mayer) gives the studio analogue: a demo or call-response relation can carry the identity that later polishing accidentally spends.

## Hypothesis

Musical material has an identity budget. Composition becomes more controllable when the composer explicitly decides which layers are invariant, which layers can vary, and which layers should be semi-fragile.
