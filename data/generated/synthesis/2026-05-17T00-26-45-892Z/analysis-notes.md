# Analysis Notes

## Connection

The strongest cross-source connection was **relation survival**: useful musical identity often persists when raw coordinates are discarded, as long as the right relation is preserved.

- S1 gives the direct harmonic anchor: Tonnetz and chord systems can be represented as combinatorial configurations and graph-like incidence structures.
- S5 gives the physical/acoustic anchor: EDM and Gram-matrix structure reduce localization from direct coordinate search to relational distance geometry.
- S3 separates static timbre identity from dynamic prosody, suggesting a musical split between source identity and performed contour.
- S4 separates noise-invariant encoder representations from degradation-adaptive decoder behavior, giving a model for invariant skeleton plus adaptive surface.
- S6 decomposes full-spectrum animal calls into decorrelated frequency-band embeddings and fuses them, preserving cross-band relation without collapsing everything into baseband.
- S2 supplies the path constraint: mathematical possibility is larger than physically reachable transformation.

## Hypothesis

If a variation deliberately discards one surface coordinate system while preserving a named relation, listeners should hear stronger continuity than in a variation where the same amount of surface change occurs without preserving that relation.

## Studio Test

Create four variations from a four-bar seed: voice-leading adjacency, spatial distance pattern, identity/contour relation, and multi-band relation. Keep tempo, form length, seed rhythm, and loudness constant. Score recognizability, transformation, and relation clarity.

## Caveat

The sources do not prove a universal law of musical perception. The connection is operational: relation-preserving representations are repeatedly useful across the selected sources, so they are worth testing as a compositional variable.
