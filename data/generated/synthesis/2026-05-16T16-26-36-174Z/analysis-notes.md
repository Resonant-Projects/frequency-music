# Analysis Notes

## Connection

The strongest cross-source connection was the **anticipatory trace**: useful information about an event can be distributed before the event arrives.

- S4 gives the clearest acoustic anchor: a 3-second window can carry severity-selective prosodic precursors for clinically severe stuttering events.
- S6 broadens the feature vocabulary with F0, MFCC, STFT, and temporal-memory fusion for short nonstationary sounds.
- S1 gives the direct musical mechanism: call-and-response as a placed incompletion that makes an answer legible.
- S2 suggests that subsurface phase/frequency-domain relations can carry structured information beneath overt perception.
- S5 reframes absence as useful information via off-screen audio generation.
- S3 adds the path-dependence constraint: arrivals should be nearest accessible states from the process path, not just theoretically strong endpoints.

## Hypothesis

If the arrival is held constant and only the preceding three-second cue-field changes, listeners should rate arrivals preceded by meaningful cue-fields as more earned and inferable than arrivals preceded by neutral material.

## Studio Test

Use four identical arrivals after four different cue-fields: prosodic, call-response, spectral, and off-screen source implication. Score earnedness, surprise, and inferability on 1-5 scales.

## Caveat

The connection is compositional and operational, not a claim that music literally shares mechanisms with stuttering prediction, watermarking, or ice crystallography. The shared structure is temporal/path-dependent information distribution.
